## About the Northern District of California

The Northern District of California is one of four federal districts in the state. It covers 15 counties: Alameda, Contra Costa, Del Norte, Humboldt, Lake, Marin, Mendocino, Monterey, Napa, San Benito, San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Santa Cruz, and Sonoma. ## The court, the judge, and the type of case

The case is assigned to Magistrate Judge Ajay S. Krishnan. A **magistrate judge** is a federal judicial officer appointed by a court's district judges for a term of years. In the Northern District, new civil cases are often assigned to magistrate judges, but a magistrate judge can preside over the whole case only if every party consents. If any party declines, the case is reassigned to a **district judge**, who is appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate.

That consent rule comes from 28 U.S.C. § 636(c). Each party files a form stating whether it consents or declines, and a party is free to decline without any adverse consequence.

The **nature of suit** is "Other Civil Rights" (440). Nature-of-suit codes are statistical categories from the civil cover sheet; they do not define the claims. The cause of action is **42 U.S.C. § 1983**, the federal law that allows a person to sue someone who, acting under color of state law, allegedly violated his federal rights. The defendant is Captain G. Serrato.

### How § 1983 claims work, in general

A § 1983 plaintiff generally must allege that a right secured by the Constitution or federal law was violated, and that the violation was committed by someone **acting under color of state law**, typically a government official exercising authority the state gave them. Officials sued in their individual capacity may raise **qualified immunity**, a doctrine that shields government officials from damages unless they violated a clearly established right that a reasonable official would have known. ## The procedural steps, explained

### IFP and service by the U.S. Marshals

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1915, a person who cannot afford the $405 filing fee may apply to proceed IFP. The court reviews the applicant's finances and screens the complaint; under § 1915(e)(2), it must dismiss the case if, among other grounds, the complaint fails to state a claim. When IFP is granted, § 1915(d) and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 4(c)(3) provide for court officers, typically the U.S. Marshals Service, to serve process. That is why the Newanforbi docket shows a Marshals service packet.

### Amending a complaint

Under Rule 15(a)(1), a plaintiff may generally amend a complaint once without permission within certain time limits, including before a responsive pleading or motion is served. An amended complaint supersedes the earlier one, so later motions are directed at the First Amended Complaint.

### Related cases in the Northern District

Civil Local Rule 3-12 requires parties to notify the court of cases that concern substantially the same parties, property, transaction, or event, and provides a procedure for asking whether cases should be related. When cases are related, the later-filed case is generally reassigned to the judge handling the earliest-filed one.

### Motions to dismiss and to strike

A motion to dismiss for failure to state a claim is brought under Rule 12(b)(6). Under *Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly* (2007) and *Ashcroft v. Iqbal* (2009), a complaint must include enough facts to make a claim plausible; the court accepts well-pleaded facts as true but not legal conclusions, and it reads self-represented filings liberally. A motion to strike under Rule 12(f) targets material that is redundant, immaterial, impertinent, or scandalous. Under Civil Local Rule 7-2, a noticed motion is generally set for hearing at least 35 days after filing.

## Representing yourself in federal court

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1654, a person may represent himself in federal court. Self-represented litigants must follow the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure and the Northern District's Civil Local Rules, including motion deadlines. There is generally no right to an appointed lawyer in a civil case, although the court may request volunteer counsel in some cases.

The Northern District's website has a "Representing Yourself" section with a Pro Se Handbook, forms, and templates. Free Legal Help Centers, run by the Justice & Diversity Center of the Bar Association of San Francisco, serve cases heard in San Francisco and Oakland, including at the Oakland federal courthouse; the Asian Law Alliance's Federal Pro Se Program serves the San Jose courthouse. They offer limited-scope advice, not representation. Because the Newanforbi case is in Oakland, the Oakland Legal Help Center is the location that serves that courthouse.
