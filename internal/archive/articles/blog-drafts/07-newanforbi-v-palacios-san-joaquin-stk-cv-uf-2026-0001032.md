## The court and the type of case

The **Superior Court** is California's general trial court; each of the state's 58 counties has one. Unlike the federal cases elsewhere in this series, this case is in state court, which has its own procedures and vocabulary.

The Superior Court of California, County of San Joaquin, hears civil, criminal, family, probate, and juvenile matters for the whole county, with its main courthouse in downtown Stockton and additional court locations elsewhere in the county. The letters in the Newanforbi case number begin with "STK" for Stockton and "CV" for civil; the remaining codes are the court's internal classifications, which the record reviewed does not explain.

### Pleading in California state court

California is often described as a "fact pleading" state. Under Code of Civil Procedure § 425.10, a complaint must contain a statement of the facts constituting the cause of action in ordinary and concise language. That differs in emphasis from the federal "plausibility" standard, although both systems test whether the complaint alleges enough to state a claim.

Some of these terms deserve explanation:

- A **complaint** is the document that starts the lawsuit. In California, **unlimited civil** cases are those seeking more than $35,000; smaller claims are "limited civil" cases with simpler procedures.
- The **summons** notifies each defendant of the suit and the deadline to respond. The **civil case cover sheet** is a standard form that helps the court classify a new case.
- A **fee waiver** is the state-court counterpart to federal "in forma pauperis" status. It excuses court fees for people who qualify financially.
- A **proof of service** is a sworn form confirming the papers were delivered. **Personal service** means the documents were handed directly to the person being served or an authorized recipient.
- A **case management conference** is a meeting with the judge about the status and scheduling of a case.
- A **demurrer** is California's version of a motion to dismiss. It challenges whether the complaint, assuming its factual allegations are true, states a legally valid claim. A judge who agrees **sustains** the demurrer; a judge who disagrees **overrules** it.
- A **reply** is the moving party's final brief responding to the opposition.
- Many California courts issue a **tentative ruling** before a hearing, telling the parties how the judge is inclined to rule. Parties can usually request **oral argument** to address the judge before the ruling becomes final.

### More on each step

**Fee waivers.** California fee waivers are governed by Government Code § 68630 and following sections and are requested on Judicial Council form FW-001. A granted waiver can cover filing fees and certain other court costs.

**Service and response time.** A California summons generally gives a defendant 30 days after service to file a response. Personal service must be made by an adult who is not a party to the case.

**Demurrer standards.** The grounds for a demurrer are listed in Code of Civil Procedure § 430.10. A demurrer is limited to the face of the complaint and matters the court may judicially notice. Before filing one, the demurring party generally must meet and confer with the plaintiff under § 430.41. When a court sustains a demurrer, it generally grants leave to amend if there is a reasonable possibility that the defect can be cured; the plaintiff bears the burden of showing how.

**Tentative rulings.** California Rule of Court 3.1308 lets superior courts adopt tentative-ruling procedures. Under a common procedure, a party who wants to argue must notify the court and the other side by a deadline, otherwise the tentative ruling becomes the court's order.

## July 15, 2026 minute order

A **minute order** is the court's official written record of what happened at a hearing. According to the [July 15, 2026 minute order on the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/brendan-ngehsi-newanforbi-v-palacios-minute-order-demurrer-san-joaquin-stk-cv-uf), Newanforbi appeared pro se and requested oral argument. **Pro se** means representing oneself; California courts also use "in propria persona," or "pro per."

The court affirmed its tentative ruling. It agreed with the defendants that exhaustion of administrative remedies was a jurisdictional requirement, found that Newanforbi had not met it before filing the First Amended Complaint, and sustained the demurrer **without leave to amend**. A formal order was signed the same day.

**Exhaustion of administrative remedies** is a rule that, in some kinds of disputes, a person must complete an available agency or administrative process before going to court. Calling it **jurisdictional** means the court treats it as a limit on its power to hear the case. Sustaining a demurrer **without leave to amend** means the court did not allow the plaintiff to file another amended complaint to try to fix the problem.

## Representing yourself in California court

Californians may represent themselves in civil cases, and courts use the terms "self-represented," "pro per," and "in propria persona." Self-represented parties are generally held to the same rules of procedure and evidence as attorneys. Newanforbi appeared pro se at the July 15, 2026 hearing, according to the minute order.

Every California superior court has a self-help center offering free information and help with forms, although the types of cases each one assists with vary. The Judicial Council's online California Courts Self-Help Guide explains many civil procedures. Other general resources include the county law library, local bar association lawyer referral services, legal aid organizations, and attorneys who offer limited-scope representation for a specific task.
