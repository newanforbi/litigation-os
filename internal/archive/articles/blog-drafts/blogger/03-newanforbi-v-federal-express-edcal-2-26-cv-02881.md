## Glossary of terms used in this case

- **Superior Court:** California's trial court of general jurisdiction; there is one per county.
- **Removal:** a defendant's transfer of a case from state court into federal court.
- **Notice of removal:** the federal filing that accomplishes removal.
- **Diversity jurisdiction:** federal authority over disputes between citizens of different states where more than $75,000 is at stake.
- **Complete diversity:** the requirement that no plaintiff share a state of citizenship with any defendant.
- **Remand:** sending a removed case back to state court.
- **Venue:** the geographic court where a case properly belongs.
- **In propria persona ("pro per") / pro se:** representing oneself; California courts tend to say the first, federal courts the second.
- **Magistrate judge:** a federal judicial officer appointed by the district judges who handles many pretrial tasks.
- **Answer:** the defendant's written reply to the complaint, admitting or denying each allegation and stating defenses.
- **Pretrial scheduling order:** the Rule 16 order that sets the case's remaining deadlines.
- **Joinder:** bringing additional parties or claims into a case.
- **Initial disclosures:** basic information, such as likely witnesses and documents, that each side shares early without a formal request.
- **Discovery:** the formal process of exchanging evidence, including written questions, document requests, and depositions.
- **Motion to compel:** a request for an order forcing the other side to respond to discovery.
- **Expert disclosures:** identification of specialized witnesses and summaries of their opinions.
- **Dispositive motion:** a motion, such as one for summary judgment, asking the court to resolve all or part of the case without trial.
- **Settlement conference:** a confidential session in which a judge who is not deciding the case helps the parties explore a resolution.
- **Litigation hold:** a step that suspends routine deletion of documents and data that might matter to a lawsuit.

## Step-by-step: how the case has moved

**1. Filing in state court.** Newanforbi began the case in San Joaquin County Superior Court. The record reviewed does not show the state filing date.

**2. Removal to federal court (August 24, 2026).** Federal Express removed the case, relying on diversity jurisdiction. Removal is permitted only when the plaintiff could have filed in federal court originally. It is governed by 28 U.S.C. §§ 1441–1447. As a general matter, the defendant must file its notice within 30 days of being served and promptly tell the state court and the other parties, after which the state court may not continue unless the case comes back on remand. For diversity purposes, a corporation is a citizen of its state of incorporation and of the state where its principal place of business is located. A remand request based on a procedural flaw generally has to be filed within 30 days of the notice, while a lack of subject-matter jurisdiction may be raised at any time. From removal onward, federal rules govern, and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 81(c) sets the response time for a defendant that had not yet answered.

Because San Joaquin County belongs to the Eastern District's Sacramento division, the case number starts with "2:" rather than the Fresno prefix "1:".

**3. Referral to a magistrate judge (September 8, 2026).** In the [September 8, 2026 referral order published by Leagle](https://www.leagle.com/decision/infdco20260909832), Magistrate Judge Sean C. Riordan explained that because the plaintiff appears in propria persona, the matter had been referred to him for pretrial proceedings under the Eastern District's Local Rule 302(c)(21). The initials at the end of the case number, "DAD" and "SCR," belong to the assigned district judge and magistrate judge.

**4. The answer.** According to the scheduling order, Federal Express was served and filed an answer, which is ECF No. 4 on the docket.

**5. The scheduling conference and order (October 8–9, 2026).** The court held a status and pretrial scheduling conference over Zoom on October 8. Drawing on the parties' status reports, it then issued the [pretrial scheduling order (ECF No. 12) posted on the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/brendan-ngehsi-newanforbi-v-federal-express-pretrial-scheduling-order-edcal-2-26-cv-02881), filed October 9. Beyond the dates in the table above, the order records that Newanforbi does not contest jurisdiction or venue and does not intend to seek remand. It also asks the parties to meet and confer on dates for a settlement conference in 2027 before a different magistrate judge.

A footnote reminds both sides that they must preserve potentially relevant materials. That reminder followed Newanforbi's request for reassurance about a "litigation hold."

**6. Where things stand.** As of the order reviewed, the case is in its pretrial phase. No trial date has been set.

## What the deadlines mean in practice

The Federal Rules give parties a toolkit for discovery. Interrogatories (Rule 33) are written questions answered under oath, usually capped at 25 per party. Requests for production (Rule 34) seek documents, and requests for admission (Rule 36) ask the other side to admit or deny specific statements. Depositions (Rule 30) are sworn testimony taken outside court, generally limited to 10 per side and one seven-hour day each. Under Rule 26(b)(1), anything sought must be relevant to a claim or defense and proportional to what the case needs.

Rule 56 governs summary judgment, which is available when no genuine dispute of material fact exists. Rule 37(e) deals with electronically stored information that was lost when it should have been kept, which helps explain why the preservation footnote matters. And under Rule 16(b)(4), a schedule like this one can be changed only by court order, generally on a showing of good cause.

## Representing yourself in this court

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1654, a party may handle a federal case personally, but must comply with the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, the court's Local Rules, and every scheduling-order deadline. The Eastern District offers a "Representing Yourself (Pro Se Litigant)" page with a pro se packet, forms, and links to the Local Rules, and it periodically hosts Pro Se Help Days in Sacramento, where law students, professors, and volunteer attorneys provide short free consultations. Self-represented parties there file on paper unless the assigned judge allows electronic filing. As the September 8 order notes, Newanforbi is proceeding in propria persona.

## Questions readers ask

### Why does a magistrate judge, rather than a district judge, run the pretrial stage?

The Eastern District's Local Rule 302(c)(21) sends pretrial matters in cases with a self-represented party to a magistrate judge, as the September 8, 2026 order explains.

### Has Federal Express responded to the complaint?

Yes. The scheduling order states that it was served and filed an answer (ECF No. 4).

### What is the next deadline after the initial disclosures?

January 15, 2027, the cutoff for motions to amend the pleadings or join parties.

### Will there be a settlement conference?

The order directs the parties to meet and confer about dates for a 2027 settlement conference before a different magistrate judge.

### What was the "litigation hold" issue?

Newanforbi asked for reassurance about preservation, and a footnote in the order reminds both parties of their duty to preserve potentially relevant materials.

### What comes next in general?

Discovery, conducted on the order's timetable. Disagreements over discovery can go to the magistrate judge. After discovery and any dispositive motions, the court sets the remaining pretrial and trial dates.
