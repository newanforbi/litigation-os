# Newanforbi v. Derrick, Step by Step: The July 2026 Ruling on Motions to Dismiss and the August Order to Show Cause (E.D. Cal. 2:26-cv-00004)

Three constitutional claims, three named defendants, and two court orders signed weeks apart: that is the core of the public record in Brendan Ngehsi Newanforbi's federal case *(PS) Newanforbi v. Derrick*, No. 2:26-cv-00004-DJC-JDP (PS), in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of California. The dispute, as the court summarized it, grows out of a parole re-entry program that Newanforbi is required to attend.

The first order, issued July 24, 2026, resolved a cluster of motions and dismissed the amended complaint while giving Newanforbi fourteen days to revise it. The second, issued August 13, 2026, observed that no revision had arrived, required him to show cause, and recorded that two defendants had been dropped at his request. The pages reviewed stop there.

Below you will find those events arranged as a dated table, followed by an ordered walk-through, a glossary, general background on the legal doctrines involved, and answers to questions readers commonly raise. The "(PS)" in the caption is the Eastern District's label for a party appearing without an attorney, and Magistrate Judge Jeremy D. Peterson signed both orders discussed here.

## Step-by-step: how the case has moved

**Step 1: The amended pleading (January 2026).** According to the court's July order, Newanforbi filed his first amended complaint in January 2026. It raised three claims under 42 U.S.C. § 1983:

- retaliation in violation of the First Amendment;
- disparate treatment based on race, under the Equal Protection Clause; and
- substantive due process.

The order describes the defendants as the program's director, Newanforbi's parole officer, and that officer's supervisor, named as Katie Palacios, A. Derrick, and G. Noguchi. These descriptions reflect the court's summary of what the complaint alleges; they are not findings of fact. Under the Eastern District's Local Rule 220, an amended complaint has to stand on its own, without leaning on the earlier version.

**Step 2: Motions from both sides.** By July, several requests were pending. Derrick and Noguchi sought dismissal and also asked to quash service. Palacios filed a separate motion to dismiss. Newanforbi had asked for email notifications, for preservation of evidence and expedited discovery, and, in two separate motions, for leave to file a supplemental opposition. There was also a motion to strike.

**Step 3: The July 24, 2026 ruling (ECF No. 31).** The [RECAP collection of Newanforbi v. Derrick filings on the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/gov.uscourts.caed.477893) contains this order. The court:

- granted Derrick and Noguchi's motion insofar as it sought dismissal, but denied it insofar as it sought to quash service;
- granted Palacios's motion to dismiss;
- denied Newanforbi's request for email notifications, his motion for evidence preservation and expedited discovery, and both motions to file a supplemental opposition;
- denied the motion to strike; and
- dismissed the first amended complaint with leave to amend within fourteen days, adding that if no amended complaint arrived, the case would go forward on the Equal Protection claim against Derrick.

**Step 4: Voluntary dismissal of two defendants (ECF No. 32).** Newanforbi filed a notice voluntarily dismissing Derrick and Noguchi. In federal court, Rule 41(a)(1)(A)(i) generally lets a plaintiff do this by notice alone, with no court order, as long as the defendant has not yet answered or moved for summary judgment.

**Step 5: The August 13, 2026 order to show cause (ECF No. 33).** In the [August 13, 2026 order to show cause on CaseMine](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/6a80984cb3657096adf76e26), the court noted that Newanforbi had not filed an amended complaint and gave him fourteen days either to explain why the action should not be dismissed or to file one. The same order acknowledged the notice of voluntary dismissal and told the Clerk to terminate Derrick and Noguchi.

**Step 6: After August 13.** The pages reviewed contain nothing later.

## Glossary of terms used in this case

- **Pro se / (PS):** without a lawyer; the parenthetical tag the Eastern District adds to such cases.
- **Magistrate judge:** a judicial officer appointed by a court's district judges for a fixed term; in this district, pretrial work in self-represented cases is generally referred to one.
- **Complaint / first amended complaint:** the pleading that opens a case, and a revised version that takes its place.
- **Section 1983:** the federal law allowing suits against someone who, with state authority, allegedly violated a person's federal rights.
- **First Amendment retaliation:** a claim that a government actor took adverse action because of a person's protected speech or activity.
- **Equal Protection (race-based disparate treatment):** the Fourteenth Amendment rule that government may not treat people differently by race without adequate justification.
- **Substantive due process:** a Fourteenth Amendment doctrine guarding against certain arbitrary government conduct.
- **Motion to dismiss:** a request to end claims without trial, typically because the complaint does not state a legal claim even if its facts are assumed true.
- **Quash service:** to challenge whether a defendant was properly served with the summons and complaint.
- **Discovery / expedited discovery:** the formal exchange of evidence, and starting that exchange sooner than the rules usually permit.
- **Supplemental opposition:** an extra brief responding to a motion.
- **Motion to strike:** a request to remove material from a filing.
- **Leave to amend:** permission to file a new complaint addressing the problems the court identified; the opposite of dismissal "without leave to amend."
- **Notice of voluntary dismissal:** a plaintiff's filing that drops claims against one or more defendants.
- **Terminate:** the Clerk's docket update showing a party is no longer in the case.
- **Order to show cause:** a directive to explain, by a deadline, why the court should not take a particular step.
- **ECF No.:** the sequence number of a docket entry.

## The doctrines in general terms

None of what follows describes the court's analysis in this case; it is general background.

*Color of state law.* A § 1983 plaintiff generally must show a federal right was violated by someone using authority granted by the state. Whether a private organization qualifies as a state actor is decided case by case, and this guide takes no position on that issue here.

*Retaliation.* Courts in the Ninth Circuit usually ask three things: whether the plaintiff engaged in protected activity; whether the defendant's action would discourage a person of ordinary firmness from continuing it; and whether the protected activity was a substantial or motivating factor behind the action.

*Equal Protection.* A disparate-treatment claim generally requires facts showing different treatment from others similarly situated, combined with a discriminatory intent or purpose. Racial classifications by government draw strict scrutiny, the most demanding form of judicial review.

*Substantive due process.* Courts read this doctrine narrowly. When executive officials are involved, the Supreme Court has framed the test as whether their conduct is so arbitrary that it "shocks the conscience."

*Qualified immunity.* Individual officials facing § 1983 damages claims may invoke this defense, which applies unless they violated a clearly established right. The record reviewed does not say whether anyone raised it here.

*Pleading and amendment.* Dismissal for failure to state a claim falls under Rule 12(b)(6), and challenges to service under Rule 12(b)(5). *Twombly* (2007) and *Iqbal* (2009) require enough factual content to make each claim plausible; bare legal conclusions fall short. Self-represented pleadings are read liberally. Rule 15(a)(2) instructs courts to "freely give leave" to amend "when justice so requires," and Ninth Circuit courts generally allow pro se plaintiffs a chance to amend unless doing so would be futile. Rule 41(b), separately, permits dismissal when a plaintiff does not prosecute a case or follow a court order, which is why courts often issue an order to show cause first.

## The court and its numbering

The Eastern District of California stretches across 34 counties, from the Oregon border through the Sacramento and San Joaquin Valleys to Kern County. Its Sacramento division covers counties like Sacramento, San Joaquin, Solano, Yolo, and Placer, while Fresno covers Fresno, Kern, Tulare, Stanislaus, and others. In "2:26-cv-00004," the "2:" signals Sacramento and "26" the year; "DJC" and "JDP" are the district judge's and magistrate judge's initials. Local Rule 302(c)(21) refers pretrial matters in self-represented cases to a magistrate judge, who can rule directly on many non-dispositive issues. Absent consent from all parties under 28 U.S.C. § 636(c), a recommendation to dismiss a case outright typically goes to the district judge as findings and recommendations.

## Help for self-represented litigants

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1654, people may represent themselves in federal court, subject to the same Federal Rules, Local Rules, and court-ordered deadlines as anyone else. In the Eastern District, pro se parties file on paper unless the judge permits electronic filing. The court's "Representing Yourself (Pro Se Litigant)" page has a pro se packet, forms, and the Local Rules, and Pro Se Help Days in Sacramento periodically offer brief free consultations with law students, professors, and volunteer attorneys. County law libraries and legal aid organizations are additional resources.

## Questions readers ask

### Which claim would have survived if no amended complaint were filed?

Under the July 24 order, the case would have continued on the Equal Protection claim against Derrick.

### Why was the request to quash service denied while dismissal was granted?

The order granted Derrick and Noguchi's motion only to the extent it sought dismissal and denied the part seeking to quash service. The pages reviewed do not set out the reasoning.

### Who signed the orders?

Magistrate Judge Jeremy D. Peterson.

### What did the August 13 order give Newanforbi the option to do?

Show cause within fourteen days why the case should not be dismissed, or file an amended complaint.

### Are Derrick and Noguchi still defendants?

After Newanforbi's notice of voluntary dismissal (ECF No. 32), the August 13 order directed the Clerk to terminate them.

### What generally happens after an order to show cause?

The court reviews whatever arrives by the deadline (a response, an amended complaint, or nothing) and decides how to proceed. If a magistrate judge in this district concludes a case should be dismissed, that usually reaches the district judge as findings and recommendations, which the parties may object to.
