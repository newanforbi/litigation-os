Glossary of terms used in this case

  • Pro se: appearing for oneself without an attorney.
  • Docket: the court's list of all filings and orders in a case.
  • Complaint / First Amended Complaint: the pleading that starts a lawsuit, and the revised pleading that replaces it.
  • In forma pauperis (IFP): permission to proceed without prepaying court fees due to limited finances.
  • Summons: the document notifying a defendant of the suit and the time to respond.
  • Service of process: formally delivering the summons and complaint.
  • Marshals service packet: the paperwork the U.S. Marshals Service uses to serve a defendant for an IFP plaintiff.
  • Returned executed: proof filed by the server showing delivery was completed.
  • Errata: a filing correcting mistakes in an earlier document.
  • Notice of related case: a filing alerting the court to other cases that may share parties or events.
  • Administrative motion: in this district, a short-form request on routine matters.
  • Related cases: cases the court links, generally so one judge handles them.
  • Motion to dismiss: a request to end claims without trial, usually because the complaint fails to state a legal claim even assuming its facts are true.
  • Motion to strike: a request to remove particular material from a pleading.
  • Opposition: the brief responding to a motion.
  • Joint case management statement: the parties' written report before a case management conference.
  • Stipulation: an agreement between parties submitted for court approval.
  • Continue: to postpone.
  • Consent/declination form: the form each party files to say whether it agrees to have a magistrate judge decide the entire case.
  • Magistrate judge / district judge: a judicial officer appointed by the court's district judges for a term of years, and a judge appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate.

Step-by-step: how the case has moved

Stage 1: Filing and the fee order (June 17–29)

Newanforbi filed his complaint along with an IFP request on June 17, 2026, and the court entered its initial case management scheduling order the same day. On June 29 it ruled on the IFP motion, a summons issued, and a Marshals service packet was prepared.

Why the Marshals? Under 28 U.S.C. § 1915, someone unable to pay the $405 filing fee can ask to proceed IFP; the court considers finances and also screens the complaint, and § 1915(e)(2) requires dismissal if, among other grounds, the complaint fails to state a claim. Once IFP status is granted, § 1915(d) and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 4(c)(3) assign service to court officers, typically the U.S. Marshals Service.

Stage 2: The amended complaint (July 15)

Newanforbi filed a First Amended Complaint, together with an errata and a notice of related case. Rule 15(a)(1) generally permits one amendment without leave within set time limits, including before a responsive pleading or motion is served. Because an amended pleading supersedes the original, later motions target the First Amended Complaint.

Stage 3: Service (August 5)

The summons came back executed, showing Serrato was served on August 5, 2026.

Stage 4: Related-case requests (August 20 – September 8)

Newanforbi filed an administrative motion asking the court to consider whether cases should be related, and the court granted it on August 24. A day later he moved to relate two more cases, 3:26-cv-08271 and 3:26-cv-08272. On September 8 the court denied that motion as to 3:26-cv-08272-LJC.

The governing rule is Civil Local Rule 3-12, which requires parties to flag cases involving substantially the same parties, property, transaction, or event and provides a way to ask whether they should be related. When they are, the later-filed case generally moves to the judge who has the earliest one.

Stage 5: The motion to dismiss and/or strike (August 26–28)

Serrato moved to dismiss the First Amended Complaint and/or strike parts of it, Newanforbi filed his opposition the next day, and on August 28 the clerk reset the hearing to September 30, 2026 at 1:30 PM in Oakland.

In general terms, Rule 12(b)(6) motions test whether a complaint states a plausible claim under Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly (2007) and Ashcroft v. Iqbal (2009); well-pleaded facts are accepted, legal conclusions are not, and self-represented filings are read liberally. Rule 12(f) lets a court strike matter that is redundant, immaterial, impertinent, or scandalous. Civil Local Rule 7-2 generally sets a noticed motion for hearing at least 35 days after filing.

Stage 6: Case management (September 8–9)

A joint case management statement was filed, and the court approved a stipulation continuing the initial case management conference.

Stage 7: Toward reassignment (September 11)

Serrato filed a consent/declination form, and the clerk then issued a notice of impending reassignment to a U.S. District Judge. Under 28 U.S.C. § 636(c), a magistrate judge may preside over an entire civil case only if every party consents, and a party may decline without any adverse consequence. When a party does not consent, the case goes to a district judge.

The court and the claim type

The Northern District of California, one of four federal districts in the state, covers 15 counties, including Alameda, Contra Costa, San Francisco, and Santa Clara, and sits in San Francisco, Oakland, San Jose, and Eureka-McKinleyville. The first digit of a case number marks the courthouse ("3:" San Francisco, "4:" Oakland, "5:" San Jose), so 4:26-cv-05953 is a 2026 Oakland civil case. Local rules route new civil matters to a courthouse largely by the county where the claim arose, then assign a judge at random. The case is assigned to Magistrate Judge Ajay S. Krishnan.

The nature of suit is "Other Civil Rights" (440), a statistical code from the civil cover sheet that does not define the claims. The listed cause of action is 42 U.S.C. § 1983, which generally requires a plaintiff to allege a violation of a federal right by someone acting under color of state law, usually a government official using state-granted authority. Officials sued individually may raise qualified immunity, which shields them from damages unless they violated a clearly established right a reasonable official would have known. The docket page reviewed describes neither the claims in the First Amended Complaint nor the grounds of the motion, so this guide does not discuss how these doctrines might apply.

Reading the docket yourself

Unless sealed, federal filings are generally public. PACER, the judiciary's official system, charges $0.10 per page up to $3 per document and waives quarterly charges of $30 or less; search the Northern District for 4:26-cv-05953. PacerMonitor is a private service that republishes PACER docket data, which is why the entries can be seen without a court login. Justia dockets, CourtListener's RECAP Archive, and GovInfo may also carry material from the case. Each entry receives an ECF number in filing order, which is how documents are cited.

Resources for self-represented parties

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1654, people may represent themselves in federal court, though they must follow the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure and the Civil Local Rules. Civil litigants generally have no right to appointed counsel, although the court may request volunteer counsel in some cases. The Northern District's "Representing Yourself" section includes a Pro Se Handbook, forms, and templates. Free Legal Help Centers run by the Justice & Diversity Center of the Bar Association of San Francisco serve San Francisco and Oakland cases, including at the Oakland federal courthouse that serves this case, and the Asian Law Alliance's Federal Pro Se Program serves San Jose. They give limited-scope advice, not representation.

Why did the Marshals handle service instead of Newanforbi?

When was the motion hearing set?

The clerk reset it to September 30, 2026, at 1:30 PM in Oakland. The docket page reviewed shows no ruling.

What usually follows a notice of impending reassignment?

The clerk assigns a district judge, who may keep, reset, or vacate (cancel) earlier hearing dates. That judge then decides the pending motion (granting, denying, or partly granting it, with or without leave to amend) and typically reschedules the initial case management conference.
