# Inside Newanforbi v. State of California: Timeline, Key Filings, and What Each Step Means (E.D. Cal. 2:25-cv-01460)

Brendan Ngehsi Newanforbi's federal lawsuit over a roadside encounter with the California Highway Patrol traces back to a single early-morning date: November 1, 2024. Roughly seven months later, on May 27, 2025, he filed *(PS) Newanforbi v. State of California et al.* in the Sacramento division of the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of California, where it carries case number 2:25-cv-01460-DC-CSK. The "(PS)" prefix is how this court flags a case brought by someone without a lawyer.

Since then the case has passed through a denied IFP motion, an order to show cause, payment of the filing fee, a ruling on electronic filing, and, on April 20, 2026, a magistrate judge's recommendation that the case be dismissed and closed. That recommendation is not itself a final ruling, and the pages reviewed do not show what the district judge did with it.

## The case in five stages

**Stage 1: What the complaint says.** The court's April 2026 Findings and Recommendations recount the complaint's version of events. According to that summary, around 1 a.m. on November 1, 2024, Newanforbi was parked on a dirt shoulder off I-580 in Livermore when two CHP officers came up to the car, looked into a suspected DUI, searched the vehicle, and detained him. On that basis he pleaded claims under the Fourth Amendment, the Fourteenth Amendment, and *Monell*. Everything in this paragraph is an allegation; none of it is a court finding.

The named defendants are the State of California, the California Highway Patrol, Santiago Meza-Gonzalez, and Melano. The court lists the nature of suit as "Civil Rights: Other," and the cause of action as 42 U.S.C. § 1983.

**Stage 2: The fee question (August–November 2025).** In August 2025 the court turned down Newanforbi's request to proceed without prepaying fees, giving him 30 days to try again; that ruling is the [GovInfo copy of the August 25, 2025 order denying IFP](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCOURTS-caed-2_25-cv-01460/USCOURTS-caed-2_25-cv-01460-0). In October the court issued an [order to show cause, posted on GovInfo as a PDF](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-caed-2_25-cv-01460/pdf/USCOURTS-caed-2_25-cv-01460-1.pdf), requiring him within 14 days either to pay or to submit a renewed application. He paid the $405 fee on October 28, 2025, summonses went out that day, and the court withdrew the order to show cause on November 3.

Paying the fee changed who handles service. A plaintiff who pays is generally responsible for getting the summons and complaint to each defendant under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 4, and Rule 4(m) usually allows 90 days from filing to do it.

**Stage 3: Filing logistics (November 17, 2025).** Newanforbi asked to file through CM/ECF, the federal courts' electronic system. The court said no to that but allowed him to receive court notices electronically. In this district, self-represented parties file on paper unless the assigned judge grants permission otherwise.

**Stage 4: The motion to dismiss and the recommendation (April 20, 2026).** The defendants moved to dismiss (ECF No. 12). Magistrate Judge Chi Soo Kim answered with [Findings and Recommendations available on GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USCOURTS-caed-2_25-cv-01460/USCOURTS-caed-2_25-cv-01460-2) (ECF No. 23), recommending that the motion be granted, the complaint be dismissed without leave to amend, and the case be closed. The document notes that Newanforbi had agreed to dismissal of the State of California, of the *Monell* claims against CHP, and of the official-capacity claims against the officers. It set a 14-day window for objections. A second copy is the [Internet Archive posting of the Findings and Recommendations](https://archive.org/details/brendan-ngehsi-newanforbi-v-state-of-california-findings-and-recommendations-edcal-2-25-cv-01460).

**Stage 5: Awaiting the district judge.** Under 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1), Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 72(b), and the Eastern District's Local Rule 304, a magistrate judge's proposed disposition goes to the district judge, who looks afresh (de novo) at any portion a party objected to and may adopt, reject, or modify it. In the Ninth Circuit, skipping objections can narrow what a party may argue on appeal, which is why these documents typically carry a warning about it. The pages reviewed do not show a decision by District Judge Dena M. Coggins.

## Glossary of terms used in this case

- **(PS):** the Eastern District's marker for a pro se matter, meaning a party appearing without counsel.
- **Section 1983:** the federal statute allowing suits against people who, acting with state authority, allegedly violated someone's federal rights.
- **Fourth Amendment:** the constitutional protection against unreasonable searches and seizures.
- **Fourteenth Amendment:** guarantees due process and equal protection against actions by states.
- ***Monell* claim:** named for a 1978 Supreme Court decision; it tries to hold a government entity itself liable based on its policy or custom, instead of only its individual employees.
- **IFP (in forma pauperis):** permission to litigate without paying fees in advance because of limited means.
- **Order to show cause:** a directive to take a step, or explain why the court should not act (for example, by dismissing).
- **Discharged:** withdrawn; the order no longer requires anything.
- **Summons:** the notice telling a defendant about the suit and the response deadline.
- **CM/ECF:** the federal judiciary's electronic filing platform; self-represented parties generally need leave to use it.
- **Electronic service:** receiving the court's notices electronically rather than by mail.
- **Motion to dismiss:** a request to end a case without trial, usually on the theory that the complaint states no legal claim even accepting its facts.
- **Without leave to amend:** no further chance to rewrite the complaint.
- **Official capacity vs. individual capacity:** a claim against the office (treated as a claim against the government) versus a claim against the person.
- **Findings and recommendations (F&R):** a magistrate judge's proposed resolution of a matter that the magistrate judge cannot finally decide alone.
- **Objections:** written challenges to an F&R, filed within the deadline.

## Legal background, in general terms

The Fourth Amendment requires searches and seizures to be reasonable. Courts typically measure a short investigative stop against "reasonable suspicion" and an arrest against the stricter "probable cause." The Fourteenth Amendment's Due Process Clause guards against losing life, liberty, or property without fair procedure, and its Equal Protection Clause generally demands similar treatment of people in similar situations.

Two general rules shape who can be sued under § 1983. Under *Monell*, a local government is not liable just because it employs a wrongdoer; the harm must be tied to a policy, custom, or comparable decision. And the Supreme Court has held that states and state agencies are not "persons" subject to damages suits under § 1983. These principles explain why § 1983 litigation often turns on whom a plaintiff sues and in what capacity. The court's own reasoning in this case is in the F&R linked above.

On a motion under Rule 12(b)(6), the question is whether the complaint, read with its well-pleaded facts taken as true, states a plausible claim, the standard from *Twombly* (2007) and *Iqbal* (2009). Legal conclusions get no such presumption, and pleadings drafted by self-represented parties are construed liberally.

## About this court

The Eastern District of California spans 34 counties, reaching from the Oregon line down through the Sacramento and San Joaquin Valleys to Kern County. Its Sacramento division takes cases from counties such as Sacramento, San Joaquin, Solano, Yolo, Placer, El Dorado, Shasta, and Butte; Fresno handles counties such as Fresno, Kern, Tulare, Stanislaus, Merced, and Madera. Courthouses also operate in Redding, Bakersfield, and Yosemite. A "2:" prefix marks Sacramento (Fresno uses "1:"), "25" is the year, and "DC" and "CSK" are the judges' initials.

Two judges appear because Local Rule 302(c)(21) refers pretrial work in self-represented cases to a magistrate judge. District judges are nominated by the President, confirmed by the Senate, and hold life tenure; magistrate judges are appointed by the district judges for set terms.

## Self-representation resources

Federal law, 28 U.S.C. § 1654, permits people to represent themselves, but they remain bound by the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure and local rules. The Eastern District's "Representing Yourself (Pro Se Litigant)" page offers a pro se packet, forms, and the Local Rules, and the court periodically holds Pro Se Help Days in Sacramento, where law students, professors, and volunteer lawyers give brief free consultations. Lawyer referral services, legal aid groups, and county law libraries are other options.

## Questions readers ask

### Why did the court issue an order to show cause about fees?

After the August 2025 denial, the October 17 order required Newanforbi to pay or to reapply within 14 days. He paid on October 28, and the order was discharged on November 3.

### Which claims did Newanforbi agree to drop?

According to the F&R, he consented to dismissal of the State of California, the *Monell* claims against CHP, and the official-capacity claims against the officers.

### Does "without leave to amend" mean the case is finished?

Not by itself. It is part of a recommendation, and the pages reviewed do not show the district judge's action on it.

### Can Newanforbi file electronically?

The November 17, 2025 order denied use of CM/ECF but granted electronic service of court notices.

### Is there a companion state case?

Yes. A separate post in this series covers Newanforbi's Alameda County complaint about the same November 1, 2024 events.

### What usually follows an F&R?

Once objections are in or the deadline passes, the district judge enters an order adopting, rejecting, or modifying it. A dismissal and closure would lead to a judgment, and a final judgment can generally be appealed to the Ninth Circuit within the deadlines the federal rules set.
