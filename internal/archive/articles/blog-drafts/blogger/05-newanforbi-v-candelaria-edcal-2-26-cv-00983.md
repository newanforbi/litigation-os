# Newanforbi v. Candelaria: A Reader's Guide to the DMV Hearing Case and the June 2026 Findings and Recommendations (E.D. Cal. 2:26-cv-00983)

How could Brendan Ngehsi Newanforbi meet the financial test for proceeding without prepaying fees and still face a recommendation that his request be denied? His federal case against a DMV hearing officer and the DMV's director shows how that happens. In *(PS) Newanforbi v. Candelaria*, No. 2:26-cv-00983-DC-CSK (PS), filed March 19, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of California, the magistrate judge found that Newanforbi had made the required financial showing. The same document nonetheless recommended denying his in forma pauperis motion, dismissing his amended complaint without leave to amend, and closing the case, because federal fee review also includes a look at the complaint itself.

That June 15, 2026 recommendation is the latest event in the pages reviewed, and those pages do not show whether the district judge adopted it. The "(PS)" tag signals that Newanforbi is representing himself. Magistrate Judge Chi Soo Kim and District Judge Dena M. Coggins are the two judges associated with the case.

This guide begins with the underlying administrative events, then sets out the federal timeline, defines key terms, and closes with reader questions and the sources.

## Background: the administrative events

The Findings and Recommendations summarize Newanforbi's First Amended Complaint (FAC). According to that summary, his Class A commercial driver's license was suspended following a November 1, 2024 arrest. On January 24, 2025, Diana Candelaria, a DMV Driver Safety Hearing Officer, conducted an Administrative Per Se hearing, and on February 11, 2025 she issued a decision upholding a one-year suspension. These are the court's descriptions of the pleading's allegations, not findings.

## Glossary of terms used in this case

- **(PS) / pro se:** a party proceeding without a lawyer, as flagged in Eastern District captions.
- **District judge:** a federal trial judge nominated by the President, confirmed by the Senate, and appointed for life.
- **Magistrate judge:** a judicial officer appointed by the district judges who handles much of the pretrial work.
- **Administrative Per Se (APS) hearing:** a California DMV proceeding, run by a DMV hearing officer rather than a judge, about whether a license should be suspended after a DUI-related arrest; it is separate from any criminal case.
- **First Amended Complaint (FAC):** the revised pleading that replaced the original complaint.
- **42 U.S.C. § 1983:** the federal statute allowing suits against those who, with state authority, allegedly violated someone's federal rights.
- **Procedural due process:** the constitutional right to fair procedures, such as notice and a meaningful hearing, before the government removes a protected interest.
- **Declaratory relief:** a request that the court declare the parties' legal rights.
- **Injunctive relief:** a request for an order requiring someone to act or to stop acting.
- **Individual vs. official capacity:** suing a person personally, versus suing in a way the law treats as a claim against the government office.
- **In forma pauperis (IFP):** proceeding without prepaying the filing fee because of limited finances.
- **Without prejudice:** a ruling that does not prevent a later attempt.
- **Quasi-judicial immunity:** protection from personal damages suits for officials performing judge-like functions, such as conducting hearings.
- **Eleventh Amendment immunity:** the general bar, subject to exceptions, on federal suits against a state, its agencies, and state officials sued in their official capacities.
- **Failure to state a claim:** the complaint, even taken as true, does not set out a legal basis for relief.
- **Without leave to amend:** no further amended complaint permitted in this case.
- **Objections:** a party's written challenge to findings and recommendations.

## Step-by-step: how the case has moved

**1. The filing (March 19, 2026).** Newanforbi started the federal action and asked to proceed IFP.

**2. A first denial (April 22, 2026).** The court rejected the IFP application because the supporting affidavit was insufficient. Because the denial was without prejudice, he remained free to reapply.

**3. Renewed application and amended pleading (April 29, 2026).** One week later he submitted a renewed IFP application and the FAC. The FAC pleads three claims:

- procedural due process under § 1983, against Candelaria individually;
- declaratory relief, against DMV Director Steve Gordon in his official capacity; and
- injunctive relief, also against Gordon in his official capacity.

**4. Screening and the recommendation (June 15, 2026).** In the [Findings and Recommendations on Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/caedce/2:2026cv00983/484817/6) (ECF No. 6, shown on Justia as "Document 6"), Magistrate Judge Kim accepted that Newanforbi had satisfied the financial requirement. The recommendation nevertheless was that the IFP motion be denied, the FAC be dismissed without leave to amend, and the Clerk close the case.

Three grounds are given: quasi-judicial immunity shielding the hearing officer, Eleventh Amendment immunity barring the official-capacity claims, and failure to state a constitutional claim. The recommended dismissal is "without prejudice to filing an appropriate action in state court." Objections were due within 14 days of service. ## Why the fee request and the complaint are linked

Federal IFP review has two halves. The court examines the applicant's finances, and it also screens the complaint. Under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2), it must dismiss an IFP case if, among other grounds, the complaint fails to state a claim or seeks money from a defendant who is immune. That screening uses the same yardstick as a Rule 12(b)(6) motion: per *Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly* (2007) and *Ashcroft v. Iqbal* (2009), the factual allegations must make a claim plausible, and legal conclusions are not presumed true. Pro se pleadings are construed liberally, and a chance to amend is usually allowed unless amendment would be futile.

## Two phrases that seem to clash

"Without leave to amend" and "without prejudice" can look inconsistent, but they answer different questions. The first concerns whether Newanforbi could submit yet another amended complaint in this federal case. The second, as the recommendation uses it, concerns whether he may bring an appropriate action somewhere else, which the document identifies as state court. This guide does not suggest what action, if any, would be appropriate.

## Procedural due process, generally

A procedural due process claim usually involves two questions: did the plaintiff have a protected liberty or property interest, and were the procedures used constitutionally sufficient? To gauge how much process is required, courts often turn to *Mathews v. Eldridge* (1976), weighing the private interest, the risk of an erroneous deprivation under current procedures along with the value of added safeguards, and the government's interest. The court's actual reasoning about the FAC is in the linked document.

## The review process for a recommendation

A magistrate judge's findings and recommendations are a proposal, not a final decision. Any party can object in writing, and the district judge then reviews the contested portions de novo (afresh, without deference) and accepts, rejects, or modifies the proposal. The rules come from 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1), Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 72(b), and Eastern District Local Rule 304. In the Ninth Circuit, not objecting can restrict which issues may later be raised on appeal. The Eastern District's Local Rule 302(c)(21) is what refers self-represented cases to a magistrate judge for pretrial matters; when dismissal is warranted and the parties have not consented to the magistrate judge deciding everything, the result is an F&R directed to the district judge.

## About the Eastern District

The district spans 34 counties, from the Oregon border through the Sacramento and San Joaquin Valleys to Kern County. Sacramento hears matters from counties such as Sacramento, San Joaquin, Solano, Yolo, and Placer; Fresno handles counties such as Fresno, Kern, Tulare, and Stanislaus. "2:" in this case number denotes Sacramento, and the closing "DC" and "CSK" are the judges' initials.

## Help for people without lawyers

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1654, individuals may represent themselves in federal court, but the Federal Rules and Local Rules apply to them fully, and in this district they file on paper unless the judge allows electronic filing. The court's "Representing Yourself (Pro Se Litigant)" page provides a pro se packet, forms, and the Local Rules, and periodic Pro Se Help Days in Sacramento offer short free consultations. For state-court matters, every California superior court runs a self-help center (each assists with different case types), and the Judicial Council's online California Courts Self-Help Guide covers many civil subjects.
