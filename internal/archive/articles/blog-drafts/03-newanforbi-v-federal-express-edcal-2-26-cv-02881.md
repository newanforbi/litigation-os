## About the Eastern District of California

The Eastern District of California covers 34 counties in the Central Valley and the eastern and northern parts of the state. Its Sacramento division hears cases arising in counties including Sacramento, San Joaquin, Solano, Yolo, Placer, and Shasta, and its Fresno division hears cases from counties including Fresno, Kern, Tulare, Stanislaus, and Merced. A case removed from San Joaquin County Superior Court lands in the Sacramento division, which is why the Newanforbi case number begins with "2:" (Sacramento) rather than "1:" (Fresno).

## What "removal" means

A California **Superior Court** is the state's general trial court; each county has one. **Removal** is the process by which a defendant moves a case that was filed in state court into federal court. It is allowed only when the case could have been filed in federal court in the first place, for example because it raises a federal question or because of **diversity jurisdiction**, which generally applies when the parties are citizens of different states and more than $75,000 is in controversy. A plaintiff who believes removal was improper can ask the federal court to **remand**, or send the case back to state court.

### How removal works in practice

Removal is governed by 28 U.S.C. §§ 1441–1447. A defendant generally must file a **notice of removal** in federal court within 30 days after being served, and must promptly notify the state court and the other parties. Once that happens, the state court can no longer proceed with the case unless it is remanded.

For diversity purposes, there must generally be **complete diversity**, meaning no plaintiff shares state citizenship with any defendant. A corporation is treated as a citizen both of the state where it is incorporated and of the state where it has its principal place of business. A motion to remand based on a procedural defect in removal generally must be filed within 30 days after the notice of removal, while a lack of subject-matter jurisdiction can be raised at any time. In the Newanforbi case, the scheduling order states that Newanforbi does not dispute jurisdiction and does not plan to seek remand.

After removal, Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 81(c) sets the time for a defendant that has not yet answered to respond. Federal rules, not state rules, govern the case from that point.

**In propria persona**, often shortened to "pro per," is the term California courts commonly use for a person who represents himself; federal courts more often say **pro se**. A **magistrate judge** is a federal judicial officer appointed by a court's district judges who handles many pretrial matters. Local Rule 302(c)(21) is the Eastern District rule that sends cases with a self-represented party to a magistrate judge for pretrial work. An **answer** is the defendant's formal written response to the complaint, admitting or denying each allegation and listing any defenses. Motions to compel must be heard by June 3, 2027. **Discovery** is the formal exchange of evidence, including document requests, written questions, and depositions. A **motion to compel** asks the court to order the other side to respond.
- **Expert disclosures:** Initial disclosures are due July 30, 2027 and rebuttal disclosures August 27, 2027. Expert discovery closes September 24, 2027. Expert disclosures identify witnesses with specialized knowledge and summarize their opinions.
- **Dispositive and other motions:** They must be filed by September 30, 2027. A **dispositive motion**, such as a motion for summary judgment, asks the court to decide all or part of the case without a trial.
- **Trial:** The court will set trial dates after dispositive motions are resolved or the motion deadline passes.
- **Settlement:** The parties are to meet and confer about dates for a 2027 settlement conference before a different magistrate judge. A **settlement conference** is a confidential meeting where a judge not deciding the case helps the parties explore resolution.

In a footnote, the order also reminds both parties of their obligation to preserve potentially relevant materials. Newanforbi had asked for reassurance about a "litigation hold." A **litigation hold** is a step taken to stop the routine deletion or destruction of documents and data that may be relevant to a lawsuit.

### Discovery tools in general

Federal discovery rules give each side several tools: **interrogatories** (written questions answered under oath, generally limited to 25 per party under Rule 33), **requests for production** of documents under Rule 34, **requests for admission** under Rule 36, and **depositions** (sworn out-of-court testimony, generally limited to 10 per side and one day of seven hours each under Rule 30). Discovery must be relevant to a claim or defense and proportional to the needs of the case under Rule 26(b)(1).

### Summary judgment and preservation in general

A motion for **summary judgment** under Rule 56 asks the court to rule without trial because there is no genuine dispute of material fact. Rule 37(e) addresses electronically stored information that should have been preserved but was lost, which is part of why preservation reminders like the one in the Newanforbi scheduling order matter.

## Representing yourself in federal court

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1654, parties may represent themselves in federal court, but they must follow the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure and the court's Local Rules, including deadlines set in a scheduling order. The schedule can be changed only by court order, generally on a showing of good cause under Rule 16(b)(4).

The Eastern District's "Representing Yourself (Pro Se Litigant)" page offers a pro se packet, forms, and links to the Local Rules. The court periodically hosts Pro Se Help Days in Sacramento, where law students, professors, and volunteer attorneys offer short free consultations. Self-represented litigants in the Eastern District file on paper unless the assigned judge allows electronic filing.
