## About the Northern District of California

The Northern District of California is one of four federal districts in the state. It covers 15 counties: Alameda, Contra Costa, Del Norte, Humboldt, Lake, Marin, Mendocino, Monterey, Napa, San Benito, San Francisco, San Mateo, Santa Clara, Santa Cruz, and Sonoma. Because Newanforbi filed his case in Alameda County Superior Court, it came to this district on removal: federal law sends a removed case to the district court for the district and division where the state action was pending.

Northern District case numbers start with a digit identifying the courthouse: "3:" for San Francisco, "4:" for Oakland, and "5:" for San Jose. Under the court's Civil Local Rules, cases arising in Alameda County are generally assigned to the San Francisco or Oakland courthouses, and judges are assigned at random. Many new civil cases in the district are assigned initially to magistrate judges, who can preside over the whole case only if all parties consent.

## What "removal" means

The **Alameda County Superior Court** is a California state trial court. The **U.S. District Court** is a federal trial court. **Removal** is the process by which a defendant moves a case that a plaintiff filed in state court into federal court. It is generally allowed only if the case could have been filed in federal court originally, for example because it raises a claim under federal law. The defendant starts the process by filing a **notice of removal** in federal court, generally within 30 days after being served, and notifying the state court. A plaintiff who believes removal was improper can ask the federal court to **remand**, or send the case back.

### The removal statutes in general

Removal is governed by 28 U.S.C. §§ 1441–1447, and those statutes frame the early stage of the Newanforbi case. Federal courts have "federal question" jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331 over cases arising under federal law. Once the notice is filed and the state court is notified, the state court may not proceed unless the case is remanded. A remand motion based on a procedural defect generally must be filed within 30 days after the notice of removal, but a lack of subject-matter jurisdiction can be raised at any time. After removal, Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 81(c) sets the response deadline for a defendant that has not yet answered.

### FCRA basics for employment background checks

In general terms, the FCRA includes several provisions that commonly come up in employment screening:

- **Disclosure and authorization:** Before obtaining a consumer report for employment purposes, an employer generally must give the applicant or employee a clear written disclosure, in a document consisting solely of the disclosure, and obtain written authorization (15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(2)).
- **Pre-adverse action notice:** Before taking adverse action based in whole or in part on a report, an employer generally must provide a copy of the report and a summary of the person's FCRA rights (§ 1681b(b)(3)).
- **Accuracy and disputes:** Consumer reporting agencies must follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy (§ 1681e(b)) and must reinvestigate items a consumer disputes (§ 1681i).
- **Remedies and timing:** The statute provides for damages for willful or negligent noncompliance (§§ 1681n and 1681o), and § 1681p sets the time limits for filing suit and allows FCRA cases to be brought in federal or state court.

These are general descriptions of the statute, not a description of what is alleged in the Newanforbi complaint.

### What the "Civil Rights – Employment" code generally covers

Nature-of-suit code 442 is commonly used for employment discrimination cases. In general, Title VII of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 prohibits employment discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, and national origin, and Title I of the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) prohibits disability discrimination in employment; both generally apply to employers with 15 or more employees and require filing an administrative charge, typically with the EEOC or a state agency such as California's Civil Rights Department, before suing. ## Representing yourself in federal court

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1654, a person may represent himself in federal court. Self-represented litigants must follow the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure and the Northern District's Civil Local Rules, including deadlines that begin running once a case is removed.

The Northern District's "Representing Yourself" section offers a Pro Se Handbook, forms, and templates. Free Legal Help Centers, run by the Justice & Diversity Center of the Bar Association of San Francisco, serve cases heard in San Francisco and Oakland, and the Asian Law Alliance's Federal Pro Se Program serves the San Jose courthouse. They offer limited-scope advice, not representation. Newanforbi is proceeding pro se in this case.

## What happens next

In general, after a case is removed to the Northern District of California, it is assigned to a judge, and the court issues an initial order setting a case management schedule. A defendant that has not already answered must respond to the complaint within the time set by the federal rules, either with an **answer** (a written response admitting or denying the allegations) or with a **motion to dismiss** (a request to end some or all claims without trial). A plaintiff who wants to challenge the removal on procedural grounds generally must move to remand within 30 days after the notice of removal is filed. The parties then usually meet and confer and file a joint **case management statement** before a **case management conference** with the judge.
