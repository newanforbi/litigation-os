About the Eastern District of California

The Eastern District of California covers 34 counties, from the Oregon border through the Sacramento and San Joaquin Valleys to Kern County. It has two main divisions: Sacramento, which hears cases from counties such as Sacramento, San Joaquin, Solano, Yolo, and Placer, and Fresno, which hears cases from counties such as Fresno, Kern, Tulare, and Stanislaus. The Newanforbi case number begins with "2:", the Sacramento designation, and ends with "DC" and "CSK," the initials of the district judge and magistrate judge.

The Eastern District's Local Rule 302(c)(21) refers pretrial matters in cases with a self-represented party to a magistrate judge. When a magistrate judge concludes that a case should be dismissed, and the parties have not consented to the magistrate judge deciding the whole case, the magistrate judge issues findings and recommendations to the district judge.

What the case is about

The court's Findings and Recommendations summarize the First Amended Complaint (FAC), the revised version of the complaint that replaced the original. It concerns the suspension of Newanforbi's Class A commercial driver's license after a November 1, 2024 arrest. It also concerns a January 24, 2025 Administrative Per Se hearing before Diana Candelaria, a DMV Driver Safety Hearing Officer, who issued a decision on February 11, 2025 sustaining a one-year suspension.

An Administrative Per Se (APS) hearing is a California DMV proceeding about whether a driver's license should be suspended after a DUI-related arrest. It is an administrative process, separate from any criminal case, and it is conducted by a DMV hearing officer rather than a judge.

The FAC asserted three claims:

  • a § 1983 procedural due process claim against Candelaria in her individual capacity
  • a claim for declaratory relief against DMV Director Steve Gordon in his official capacity
  • a claim for injunctive relief against Gordon in his official capacity

Some definitions: 42 U.S.C. § 1983 is the federal law that lets a person sue someone who, acting under color of state law, allegedly violated his federal rights. Procedural due process is the constitutional guarantee of fair procedures, such as notice and a meaningful hearing, before the government takes away a protected interest. Declaratory relief asks a court to state the parties' legal rights; injunctive relief asks the court to order someone to do or stop doing something. An individual-capacity claim targets a person personally, while an official-capacity claim is treated as a claim against the government office. These are the plaintiff's claims as summarized by the court, not findings.

Procedural due process in general

A procedural due process claim generally has two parts: whether the plaintiff had a protected liberty or property interest, and whether the procedures used were constitutionally adequate. To decide how much process is due, courts often apply the balancing test from Mathews v. Eldridge (1976), which weighs the private interest affected, the risk of an erroneous decision under the existing procedures and the value of additional safeguards, and the government's interest. The Findings and Recommendations contain the court's actual analysis of the Newanforbi FAC.

Findings and Recommendations (June 15, 2026)

In the Findings and Recommendations on Justia (ECF No. 6), Magistrate Judge Kim found that Newanforbi had made the required financial showing. The judge nonetheless recommended that:

  • the IFP motion be denied
  • the FAC be dismissed without leave to amend
  • the Clerk close the case

It may help to know that IFP review in federal court has two parts. The court looks at the applicant's finances, and it also screens the complaint itself. Under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2), a court must dismiss an IFP case if, among other grounds, the complaint fails to state a claim or seeks money from a defendant who is immune.

The stated reasons were quasi-judicial immunity for the hearing officer, Eleventh Amendment immunity for the official-capacity claims, and failure to state a constitutional claim. Quasi-judicial immunity protects officials who perform judge-like functions, such as conducting hearings, from personal damages suits over those functions. The Eleventh Amendment generally bars suits against a state, its agencies, and state officials sued in their official capacities in federal court, subject to exceptions. Failure to state a claim means the complaint, even taken as true, does not set out a legal basis for relief. Without leave to amend means no further revised complaint would be allowed in this case.

The recommended dismissal is "without prejudice to filing an appropriate action in state court." Objections were due within 14 days of service.

The pleading standard used in screening

When a court screens an IFP complaint for failure to state a claim, it generally applies the same standard used for a Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss. Under Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly (2007) and Ashcroft v. Iqbal (2009), the complaint must contain enough factual allegations to make a claim plausible, and legal conclusions are not accepted as true. Self-represented plaintiffs' pleadings are construed liberally, and leave to amend is generally given unless the court concludes amendment would be futile.

"Without prejudice" versus "without leave to amend"

These phrases can look contradictory but address different things. "Without leave to amend" refers to whether the plaintiff may file another amended complaint in this federal case. "Without prejudice," as used in the recommendation, refers to whether the plaintiff may bring an appropriate action elsewhere, here described as state court. This post does not suggest what action, if any, would be appropriate.

How findings and recommendations are reviewed

Findings and recommendations are a magistrate judge's proposed decision; they are not final on their own. Parties may file written objections, and the district judge reviews de novo (fresh, without deference) the parts objected to, then accepts, rejects, or modifies the recommendation.

The governing provisions are 28 U.S.C. § 636(b)(1), Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 72(b), and Eastern District Local Rule 304. In the Ninth Circuit, failing to object can limit the issues a party may raise on appeal. In the Newanforbi case, objections were due within 14 days of service.

Representing yourself in federal court

Under 28 U.S.C. § 1654, a person may represent himself in federal court. Self-represented litigants must follow the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure and the court's Local Rules, and in the Eastern District they file on paper unless the assigned judge permits electronic filing. Newanforbi's case carries the court's "(PS)" designation for self-represented matters.

The Eastern District's "Representing Yourself (Pro Se Litigant)" page provides a pro se packet, forms, and the Local Rules, and the court periodically hosts Pro Se Help Days in Sacramento with free short consultations from law students, professors, and volunteer attorneys. For state-court matters, every California superior court has a self-help center (the case types each assists with vary), and the Judicial Council's online California Courts Self-Help Guide covers many civil topics.
