# Default Judgment Damages Prompt (reusable)

Originally written for Ngwa Nforbi v. Home Depot (E.D. Cal. 2:26-cv-03310-TLN-AC). Swap in the CASE, numbers, and analogy sections when reusing for another case.

---

You are an experienced plaintiff-side employment litigator in California federal court. Build the strongest, highest defensible damages request for a motion for default judgment under FRCP 55(b)(2), construing every fact and legal ambiguity in the light most favorable to the plaintiff. On default, the well-pleaded allegations of the complaint are deemed admitted (TeleVideo Sys. v. Heidenthal, 826 F.2d 915 (9th Cir. 1987)). After making the strongest case, identify the arguments a court might use to reduce the award and how plaintiff should answer each one.

CASE
- Ngwa Nforbi v. Home Depot U.S.A., Inc., E.D. Cal. No. 2:26-cv-03310-TLN-AC. Plaintiff is pro se; Marshal service on registered agent Oct 14, 2026; answer due Nov 4, 2026; assume default entered under Rule 55(a).
- Claim: Cal. Penal Code § 290.46(j), unlawful use of Megan's Law registry information in an employment decision. Remedy, § 290.46(j)(4)(A): actual damages plus an amount up to three times actual damages (minimum $250), exemplary damages, costs.
- Admitted facts: plaintiff was hired for a warehouse position in Tracy, CA around August 2024, accepted, and began onboarding; Home Depot ran a background check and then halted onboarding with no reason given, using registry information.
- Prayer: actual damages according to proof, statutory damages under § 290.46(j)(4)(A), exemplary damages, costs, and pre- and post-judgment interest; amount in controversy pleaded as exceeding $75,000.

PLAINTIFF'S NUMBERS
- $23.00/hr × 25 hrs/week = $575/week.
- Back pay from Aug 15, 2024 to Apr 15, 2028 (1,339 days, 191.29 weeks) = $109,989.29.
- Statutory award at the full 3× multiplier = $329,967.86. Also argue the reading that the 3× award is in addition to actual damages: $109,989.29 + $329,967.86 = $439,957.15.
- Prejudgment interest, Cal. Civ. Code § 3288, 7% simple, daily: $14,132.87.
- Total: $344,100.73 (3× reading) or about $454,090 (4× reading), plus costs and 28 U.S.C. § 1961 interest.

FRONT PAY TO RETIREMENT
- Plaintiff's age: [__]; retirement age: 67.
- $29,900/year × [years remaining] = $[__]; tripled = $[__]; discount to present value at [__]%.
- Make the strongest case that lost future earnings flow from the unlawful decision and are recoverable as actual damages, including the effect of the registry-based exclusion on plaintiff's future employability.

SUPPORTING ANALOGY
- In a parallel E.D. Cal. case on the same statute (2:26-cv-02881), defendant FedEx's own notice of removal valued comparable damages using an hourly-rate × hours × weeks formula through an assumed trial date, added 104 weeks of front pay, and cited emotional-distress verdicts of $266,000–$625,000. Use a defendant's own valuation methodology as support for the reasonableness of plaintiff's figures.

TASKS
1. Argue that "according to proof" plus the >$75,000 allegation gives Home Depot fair notice of an award of this size under Rule 54(c).
2. Argue for the most plaintiff-favorable reading of the § 290.46(j)(4)(A) multiplier and full trebling given willfulness and Home Depot's default.
3. Justify the April 2028 back-pay end date and front pay to retirement.
4. Support § 3288 interest at 7%.
5. Make the case for exemplary damages under Cal. Civ. Code § 3294 based on the admitted facts.
6. Walk through each Eitel v. McCool factor in plaintiff's favor.
7. List the evidence and declaration contents that would prove up each line.
8. Give the highest award a court could plausibly grant, the most likely award, and the top three counterarguments with plaintiff's best response to each.
