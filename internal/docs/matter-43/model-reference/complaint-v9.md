# I INTRODUCTION

1. California permits the public to use information disclosed through its Megan's Law Website to protect a person at risk. Except as otherwise authorized by law, California prohibits use of that information for purposes relating to employment. Cal. Penal Code § 290.46(j)(1), (j)(2)(E).

2. This action arises from The Hertz Corporation's procurement and employment use of Plaintiff's California registry information and from HireRight, LLC's alleged preparation and furnishing of a materially misleading criminal classification of administrative motor-vehicle information.

3. On October 22, 2026, Hertz offered Plaintiff a full-time Manager Trainee position at its Palo Alto branch for $23.00 per hour. Plaintiff accepted. Hertz then ordered a HireRight background report containing a separately itemized "National Sex Offender Registry" product. Two searches returned Plaintiff's California registry entry, were marked "Client Review Required" under rules bearing Hertz's name, and contained a warning that California registry information may not be used for employment purposes except to protect a person at risk.

4. HireRight completed the full report at approximately 2:29 a.m. Pacific time on November 2, 2026. At approximately 10:19 a.m., Hertz communicated that it had made a "preliminary decision recinding the offer of employment" based on Plaintiff's background report and Hertz's eligibility criteria. Hertz identified "[Criminal Information] Alcohols related offenses-Does not meet" as information supporting its decision, stopped the onboarding process, withheld the accepted position, and placed the burden on Plaintiff to persuade Hertz to reverse the decision.

5. The November 2 decision was an adverse employment action. Hertz's use of the word "preliminary" did not restore the job, permit Plaintiff to begin work, or prevent the decision from adversely affecting his prospective employment. The decision withdrew or withheld an accepted position and halted onboarding unless and until Hertz chose to reverse itself.

6. Plaintiff seeks actual damages under the Fair Credit Reporting Act against HireRight; the statutory, actual, exemplary, civil-penalty, and preventive remedies provided by Penal Code § 290.46(j) against Hertz; and the remedies provided by California's Investigative Consumer Reporting Agencies Act against Hertz.

# IV FACTUAL ALLEGATIONS

## A Hertz Offers Plaintiff the Manager Trainee Position

15. On October 22, 2026, Hertz Talent Acquisition Partner Gloria Williams told Plaintiff by e-mail: "The team would like to extend an offer to you. Congratulations!" Hertz manager Kathleen Williams separately told Plaintiff that he should receive an offer for Manager Trainee at the Palo Alto location.

16. Hertz's written offer confirmed full-time employment as a Manager Trainee at 816 San Antonio Road, Palo Alto, California, reporting to Kathleen Williams, at $23.00 per hour. The offer described the position as an entry point to Hertz's management career path.

17. The offer stated that it was contingent on successful completion of a pre-employment MVR check, if applicable, and criminal, employment, and education background checks. It further stated that if Plaintiff's background check did not meet company standards, the contingent offer would be rescinded and the employment would be null and void.

18. Plaintiff accepted the offer through Hertz's portal. The portal displayed requisition 43711 with the status "Offer Accepted." On October 23, 2026, Hertz sent a pre-boarding e-mail stating, "We are excited you are joining the winning team!" and directed Plaintiff to complete a HireRight background request.

## B The Investigative Report Disclosure Packet

19. On October 23, 2026, Plaintiff completed HireRight's electronic consent process for Hertz. The process presented a single twelve-page document titled "Disclosure Regarding Investigative Consumer Report" (the "Disclosure Packet").

20. The first page identified the employer as "The Herz Corporation." Before the packet's only signature block, the document combined the investigative-report disclosure with notices directed to residents of multiple States, a Los Angeles ordinance notice in English and Spanish, and other material unrelated to the disclosure required for Plaintiff's California investigation.

21. A page headed "CALIFORNIA APPLICANTS/EMPLOYEES ONLY - CALIFORNIA DISCLOSURE" appeared after the signature block on page eight of the same packet. That page listed categories that could be obtained, including "National Sex Offender Registry" and "MVR Express." It was not presented as a separate standalone document and bore no separate signature.

22. Plaintiff does not allege that the words "National Sex Offender Registry" were wholly absent from the twelve-page packet. The defect is that Hertz did not provide the California investigative-report disclosure, including the nature and scope of the investigation, in the standalone document California law requires before procurement. Instead, Hertz embedded that disclosure in an omnibus packet containing unrelated state, local, federal, bilingual, and summary-of-rights material.

23. At approximately 3:04 p.m. on October 23, Hertz's "Pre Employment (Field)" account submitted HireRight order OR-102326-NH87V, package "Field (Driver Required)," for requisition 43711. The order separately itemized the "National Sex Offender Registry" product.

## C The Motor Vehicle Information

24. HireRight completed the MVR component on October 23 and furnished it to The Hertz Corporation. The MVR showed a valid California Class C driver's license and two concluded administrative actions: a suspension for refusal or failure to complete a chemical test, with driving privileges reinstated May 19, 2026, and a commercial-vehicle disqualification that cleared November 30, 2025.

25. Neither administrative action is an alcohol-related criminal conviction. The report's only conviction was an October 2024 vehicle-registration infraction carrying zero points. The report identified no alcohol-related conviction in any criminal jurisdiction searched.

26. HireRight's MVR section presented the two entries as Department of Motor Vehicles suspension and disqualification records. It did not label either MVR entry as a criminal conviction or criminal offense.

27. Hertz possessed the MVR by October 23 but continued to display Plaintiff's status as "Offer Accepted" while the remaining components were pending. Hertz did not communicate an adverse decision until the full screening package, including the registry results, was completed and routed for client review.

## D The California Registry Results

28. The California Department of Justice maintains the Megan's Law Website under Penal Code § 290.46. Plaintiff is a person whose information is displayed there.

29. The United States Department of Justice operates the Dru Sjodin National Sex Offender Public Website ("NSOPW"), a public search portal that queries participating jurisdictions' public registry websites. NSOPW does not maintain an independent public registry record for California; it displays information returned from California's public website.

30. On information and belief, HireRight, a private consumer reporting agency, lacks access to the law-enforcement-only National Sex Offender Registry maintained by the FBI. A HireRight search described as performed through the United States Department of Justice therefore used the public NSOPW pathway and returned information disclosed through California's Megan's Law Website.

31. Hertz ordered three registry subrequests under different forms of Plaintiff's name. One returned "Not in Registry" and was graded "Meets Company Standards." Two returned "In Registry" on October 30, 2026 and were graded "Client Review Required."

32. HireRight's researchers recorded that Plaintiff was listed on the sex-offender registry in California and verified the result by name and date of birth. The report warned: "Information from the California Sex Offenders' Registry may not be used for employment purposes except to protect a person at risk. See CA Penal Code Section 290.46. Only use this information to make an employment decision as expressly allowed by CA law."

33. The grades were assigned under rules identified as "The Hertz Corporation (Shell) (GL-HTZ001-001) v1.7" and "Managed Adjudication 3.0." The registry hits were not discarded or isolated from the employment process. They were marked for Hertz's review under the company standards that controlled whether Plaintiff could begin employment.

34. On information and belief, Hertz configured or adopted the screening package and adjudication rules so that a California registry result would enter Hertz's eligibility-review workflow as "Client Review Required."

## E Completion of the Report and Hertz's Adverse Employment Action

35. HireRight completed the full report at approximately 2:29 a.m. Pacific time on November 2, 2026. The overall adjudication status was "Client Review Required."

36. At approximately 10:19 a.m., Hertz transmitted a notice stating that, based on Plaintiff's background report and Hertz's eligibility criteria, Hertz had made "the Company's preliminary decision recinding the offer of employment."

37. The notice identified, as information "including" the basis for the decision: "[Criminal Information] Alcohols related offenses-Does not meet."

38. The quoted classification materially changed the character of the underlying MVR information. The source records described concluded licensing actions. The notice told Plaintiff that Hertz's background-review process treated the information as "Criminal Information" and alcohol-related "offenses," even though the report identified no alcohol-related charge or conviction.

39. On information and belief, the classification or category quoted in Hertz's notice was generated, stored, transmitted, or displayed through HireRight's reporting and adjudication systems. The language appeared after HireRight applied Hertz-named rules to order OR-102326-NH87V and completed its overall adjudication. The native category fields, mapping rules, reason codes, transmission history, and employer display are in Defendants' possession.

40. The notice invited Plaintiff to provide mitigation and stated that Hertz would delay a final decision for a specified period. But it did not preserve the status quo. Hertz communicated that it had decided to rescind the accepted offer, stopped onboarding, withheld the position, and required Plaintiff to overcome that decision before he could proceed.

41. The November 2 action adversely affected Plaintiff's prospective employment within the ordinary meaning of adverse action and within 15 U.S.C. § 1681a(k)(1)(B)(ii). Hertz's description of the decision as preliminary for purposes of its notice process does not eliminate the concrete employment consequences already imposed.

42. Plaintiff timely gathered and submitted records explaining that the DMV entries were administrative licensing actions and that no DUI charge had been filed. Hertz did not restore Plaintiff to onboarding or permit him to begin work.

43. As of November 12, 2026, Hertz had not issued a separate letter labeled "final adverse action." It nevertheless continued to withhold the accepted position and onboarding. Plaintiff remained ready, willing, and able to begin work. The absence of a later label does not erase the November 2 decision or the ongoing withholding of employment.

44. Hertz had possessed the MVR component since October 23. It took no adverse action during that interval. The adverse action followed within hours after completion of the full package, which included two California registry hits routed for client review. That sequence, the registry warnings, Hertz's rule configuration, and the notice's statement that the listed reason was information "including" the basis support the reasonable inference that Hertz reviewed and used the registry information as part of its employment decision, whether independently or together with the MVR category.

45. Penal Code § 290.46 prohibits employment use; it does not require the prohibited information to be the sole or publicly stated reason for the decision. Hertz used the information when it procured the registry product for this California job, configured the results to enter its eligibility review, reviewed or caused review of the flagged results under its company standards, and then took adverse action after the full package was completed.

## F Concrete Injury and Damages

46. Plaintiff's injury from HireRight's alleged criminal classification does not depend on whether Hertz later sends an additional letter. The alleged publication to Hertz portrayed administrative licensing records as criminal alcohol-related offenses while Hertz was deciding Plaintiff's eligibility.

47. When Plaintiff read the notice, he stopped at the words "Criminal Information." He believed Hertz had been told that he had committed or been convicted of a DUI-related offense. He felt embarrassed at the prospect of having to explain a criminal offense for which he had never been charged or convicted and worried that Hertz would think he was making excuses.

48. That night Plaintiff had difficulty falling asleep and repeatedly rehearsed how he would explain the difference between the DMV actions and a criminal offense. The next day he repeatedly reread the notice and had difficulty concentrating. His concentration improved after he gathered the underlying records and prepared a response. Plaintiff did not seek counseling, medical treatment, or medication for this distress.

49. Plaintiff spent substantial time gathering the MVR, prosecution disposition, and related records so that Hertz would have accurate information. His distress arose from being characterized to a prospective employer under a "Criminal Information" heading as having alcohol-related "offenses," not merely from uncertainty over the job.

50. The November 2 adverse action also deprived Plaintiff of the accepted position, stopped his onboarding, and withheld the opportunity to earn wages and benefits from the full-time Manager Trainee position. Plaintiff seeks economic damages caused by that action according to proof. He continues to mitigate his damages through employment and job-search efforts.

# V FIRST CAUSE OF ACTION

## Failure to Follow Reasonable Procedures to Assure Maximum Possible Accuracy

## Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 U.S.C. §§ 1681e(b) and 1681o

## Against HireRight

51. Plaintiff incorporates paragraphs 7, 9, 15 through 18, 23 through 27, and 35 through 50.

52. Plaintiff is a consumer under 15 U.S.C. § 1681a(c). HireRight is a consumer reporting agency. For order OR-102326-NH87V, HireRight prepared and furnished consumer-report information for Hertz's employment use.

53. The information furnished included the MVR Express report and the completed background report. On information and belief, it also included adjudication categories, statuses, reason codes, and employer-facing displays generated or maintained within HireRight's systems for that order.

54. A report or employer-facing category communicating that Plaintiff's record contains "[Criminal Information] Alcohols related offenses" is information bearing on his character, general reputation, and personal characteristics. It remains consumer-report information even if generated by application of a client-named rule set.

55. The classification was materially misleading. The source records were administrative licensing actions, not criminal charges or convictions. HireRight's own MVR section preserved their administrative character, and the criminal searches reported no alcohol-related charge or conviction. A reasonable employer would understand the challenged language to mean that Plaintiff had committed, been charged with, or been convicted of alcohol-related criminal offenses.

56. The classification was capable of adversely affecting employment and did so. Hertz quoted it as information supporting a decision to rescind the accepted offer, stopped onboarding, and withheld the position.

57. On information and belief, HireRight failed to follow reasonable procedures to assure maximum possible accuracy. Its reporting or adjudication system permitted DMV suspension and disqualification entries to be mapped, aggregated, or displayed as criminal alcohol-related "offenses" without source-type validation, qualification, or reconciliation against the criminal searches in the same order.

58. In the alternative, if Hertz supplied the challenged wording, HireRight's procedures remain at issue to the extent HireRight's product configuration, field mapping, adjudication engine, or employer-facing display received and furnished the criminal category as part of the report without preserving the administrative nature of the source records.

59. HireRight's negligent noncompliance caused Plaintiff concrete reputational and emotional injury. The misleading criminal characterization was published to Hertz while Hertz was evaluating Plaintiff's employment eligibility and was then quoted in Hertz's adverse-action notice.

60. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1681o, Plaintiff seeks actual damages caused by HireRight's negligent noncompliance, together with recoverable costs and reasonable attorney's fees to the extent authorized by law. Plaintiff does not seek lost wages from HireRight on the present allegations and reserves the right to seek leave to allege willfulness if discovery supplies a good-faith basis.

# VI SECOND CAUSE OF ACTION

## Unlawful Use of Megan's Law Information for Employment

## California Penal Code § 290.46(j)

## Against Hertz

61. Plaintiff incorporates paragraphs 7, 8, 13 through 18, 23, 27 through 45, and 50.

62. The two California registry results were information disclosed pursuant to Penal Code § 290.46. On information and belief, HireRight's public Department of Justice search used NSOPW to retrieve information from California's Megan's Law Website.

63. Hertz used that information for purposes relating to employment. Hertz selected and purchased the registry product for Plaintiff's California job, configured or adopted rules routing a hit into its eligibility workflow, caused the two hits to be marked "Client Review Required," and completed its eligibility review after the hits were returned.

64. The November 2 adverse action was an employment use. Hertz communicated a decision rescinding or withholding Plaintiff's accepted offer, stopped onboarding, and required Plaintiff to persuade Hertz to reverse the decision. The action remained adverse notwithstanding Hertz's use of the word "preliminary."

65. The prohibited information need not be the sole reason Hertz identified publicly. The timing, rule configuration, client-review status, warning, completion of the full report, and notice identifying the stated reason as information "including" the basis support a reasonable inference that Hertz used the registry information as part of the decision. At minimum, the information entered and was considered within the eligibility-review process that produced the adverse action.

66. Hertz's use was not to protect a person at risk and was not otherwise authorized by law. The offered Manager Trainee position did not require Hertz to use information disclosed through California's public Megan's Law Website, and HireRight expressly warned Hertz against employment use except as California law permits.

67. Hertz acted intentionally or with reckless disregard of Plaintiff's rights. It selected the registry product, adopted the adjudication workflow, received the express California warning, and nevertheless permitted the results to enter and influence its employment process.

68. Under Penal Code § 290.46(j)(4)(A), Plaintiff seeks actual damages; the additional amount fixed by the trier of fact, not exceeding three times actual damages and not less than $250; and exemplary damages or, alternatively, a civil penalty not exceeding $25,000. Under § 290.46(j)(4)(B), Plaintiff seeks preventive relief against continued employment use of the information.

# VII THIRD CAUSE OF ACTION

## Nonstandalone Investigative Consumer Report Disclosure

## California Civil Code §§ 1786.16 and 1786.50

## Against Hertz

69. Plaintiff incorporates paragraphs 7, 8, 13, and 15 through 23.

70. The background report Hertz procured was an investigative consumer report under California Civil Code § 1786.2 because it contained information concerning Plaintiff's character, general reputation, personal characteristics, and mode of living for employment purposes.

71. Civil Code § 1786.16(a)(2)(B) required Hertz, before procuring the report, to provide a clear and conspicuous written disclosure in a document consisting solely of the disclosure, including the nature and scope of the investigation requested.

72. Hertz did not provide the required standalone document. The only disclosure packet presented for signature combined the investigative-report disclosure with notices for multiple States, a local ordinance in two languages, federal summary-of-rights material, New York law, San Francisco notices, and other content. The California page describing the nature and scope appeared unsigned after the packet's signature block.

73. The violation was completed before procurement. Hertz obtained Plaintiff's authorization through a noncompliant omnibus packet and then procured MVR, criminal, and registry information. The fact that page eight mentioned a registry category does not satisfy the separate statutory command that Hertz provide the disclosure in a document consisting solely of that disclosure.

74. The violation caused concrete injury. Hertz procured and used sensitive background information without first providing the legally required standalone disclosure, thereby invading the privacy and informed-consent interests protected by the statute. The resulting report entered an adverse employment decision that withdrew or withheld Plaintiff's accepted position.

75. Hertz's violation was willful or grossly negligent. Hertz is a sophisticated national employer that routinely procures investigative consumer reports concerning California applicants. The packet's structure was not an accidental stray phrase; it was a standardized twelve-page workflow combining the required disclosure with extensive extraneous material.

76. Under Civil Code § 1786.50, Plaintiff seeks actual damages or $10,000, whichever is greater, together with punitive damages, recoverable costs, and reasonable attorney's fees to the extent authorized by law.
