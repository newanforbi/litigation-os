# Matter 32: Rule 41(a)(1)(B) two-dismissal risk

**Prepared:** Nov. 21, 2026. **Matter:** 32, *Nforbi v. Meza-Gonzalez*, N.D. Cal. No. 3:26-cv-08271-CRB.
**Related:** Matter 1 (E.D. Cal. 2:25-cv-01460-DC-CSK), Matter 2 (Alameda Sup. Ct. 26CV168913).
**Status:** Internal work product. Do not file or send.

## Bottom line

**The risk is low to moderate. The defense is sound but rests on one statute and a one-day margin.** Under California law, the Alameda dismissal took effect when the clerk entered it on **Aug. 21, 2026**. The E.D. Cal. Rule 41(a)(1)(A)(i) notice (Dkt. 28) was **filed Aug. 20, 2026** and is self-executing on filing (*Commercial Space Mgmt. Co. v. Boeing Co.*, 193 F.3d 1074, 1077–78 (9th Cir. 1999)). That makes the federal notice the *first* voluntary dismissal, not the second. Rule 41(a)(1)(B) turns only a *later* notice of dismissal in federal court into an adjudication on the merits. California has no two-dismissal rule. So neither dismissal is with prejudice.

**One residual risk remains.** Defendants could argue that "previously dismissed" refers to when the plaintiff *acted*, meaning the request was received on Aug. 14 at 11:31 a.m., **six days** before the federal notice. Code Civ. Proc. § 581d answers that argument. Two fallback arguments are below.

**Strategy:** Do not raise this issue. It is Defendants' affirmative defense (Fed. R. Civ. P. 8(c), res judicata), they have not raised it, and they cannot raise it for the first time in a reply. Keep the record ready in case they raise it later.

## The rule

Fed. R. Civ. P. 41(a)(1)(B): "Unless the notice or stipulation states otherwise, the dismissal is without prejudice. But if the plaintiff previously dismissed any federal- or state-court action based on or including the same claim, a notice of dismissal operates as an adjudication on the merits."

The Ninth Circuit's elements are stated in *Rose Court, LLC v. Select Portfolio Servicing, Inc.*, 119 F.4th 679 (9th Cir. 2024). As reported in search excerpts, I have not read the full opinion; pin cites to follow:
1. the plaintiff voluntarily dismissed an action in state or federal court;
2. **thereafter**, the plaintiff voluntarily dismissed a second action pending in federal court;
3. the two dismissals concerned the same claim, using a transactional, res judicata-style test; and
4. the plaintiff seeks to raise the twice-dismissed claim again in federal court.

Other points from *Rose Court*, per the excerpts: generally only defendants named in both earlier dismissed actions may invoke the rule. Here Meza-Gonzalez and Melano were named in both, so this limit does not help.

*Lake at Las Vegas Investors Grp. v. Pac. Malibu Dev. Corp.*, 933 F.2d 724 (9th Cir. 1991): parties who are "substantially the same" may invoke the rule. A N.D. Cal. order cites it for the view that the rule does not require inquiry into the circumstances of the earlier dismissals. That weakens any purely equitable argument in this circuit.

## Analysis

**Element 1 is met.** A CCP § 581(b)(1) request is unilateral, and the clerk's entry is ministerial.

**Element 3 is met.** Both actions arose from the Nov. 1, 2024 stop and named the same officers. A transactional test reaches the state-law tort theories as well.

**Element 4 is likely met,** with a small argument available. Matter 32 was filed Aug. 10, *before* either dismissal. *Rose Court*'s wording, "seeks to raise the twice-dismissed claim again," fits a later-filed action. But claim preclusion generally attaches to the first final judgment no matter which case was filed first. Treat this only as a fallback.

**Element 2 is the defense.** Is the E.D. Cal. notice a dismissal entered "thereafter"? It is not, if the state dismissal took effect Aug. 21.
- Code Civ. Proc. § 581d: a written dismissal "shall be entered in the clerk's register and is effective for all purposes when so entered." **[VERIFY quoted text against the official code. The language was confirmed through secondary sources only, and the official sites were blocked from this session.]**
- Under California law, the Alameda action was therefore dismissed on Aug. 21, one day *after* the E.D. Cal. notice. On Aug. 20 the Alameda action had not yet been dismissed, so the E.D. Cal. notice was the first dismissal and was without prejudice.
- The Aug. 21 Alameda dismissal is a state-court dismissal. Rule 41(a)(1)(B) does not govern its effect, and California has no two-dismissal rule. Element 2 requires that the *second* dismissal be in federal court.

**Counter-argument to expect:** the rule's text, "if the plaintiff previously dismissed," looks to the plaintiff's act, and CCP § 1010.6 deems an e-filed document filed on the date received (Aug. 14). Responses:
1. § 581d's "effective for all purposes when so entered" is specific and controls over the general e-filing rule.
2. A federal court looks to state law for when a state action was dismissed.
3. The rule is construed narrowly because its consequence is harsh. *Cooter & Gell v. Hartmarx Corp.*, 496 U.S. 384, 394–97 (1990), describes the rule's purpose as preventing abuse of the unilateral right to dismiss. This was a consolidation, within one week, into a single forum that was already pending (Matter 32, filed Aug. 10), not serial filing to harass.
4. Fallback: the element 4 timing point above.

**Related:** *Doe v. Marysville Joint Unified Sch. Dist.* (Cal. July 2, 2026, S283639), as reported: Rule 41(a)(1)(B) is not itself a claim-preclusion rule, and under *Semtek Int'l Inc. v. Lockheed Martin Corp.*, 531 U.S. 497 (2001), a second voluntary dismissal does not bar a later *state-court* suit on state-law claims. A cert petition (No. 26-444) is reported pending. **[UNVERIFIED: official reporter cite and cert status.]** This case matters if the Matter 2 state-law theories are ever refiled in state court. It does not control a federal court's treatment of federal § 1983 claims.
