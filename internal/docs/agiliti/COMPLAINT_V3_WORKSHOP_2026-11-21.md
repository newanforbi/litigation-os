## 2. How the discovery rule actually works here

**FCRA (§ 1681p(1)).** "Discovery of the violation" means discovering the facts that make up the violation, not realizing they are illegal. Discovery also includes facts a reasonably diligent plaintiff would have found. *Merck & Co. v. Reynolds*, 559 U.S. 633 (2010) [KNOWN] (construing parallel "discovery" language in 28 U.S.C. § 1658(b)). Courts apply *Merck* to § 1681p. *Mack v. Equable Ascent Fin., LLC*, 748 F.3d 663 (5th Cir. 2014) [UNVERIFIED]; see *Drew v. Equifax Info. Servs., LLC*, 690 F.3d 1100 (9th Cir. 2012) [UNVERIFIED — confirm what it holds on § 1681p before citing]. Background: *TRW Inc. v. Andrews*, 534 U.S. 19 (2001) [KNOWN] held the pre-2003 FCRA had no general discovery rule, and Congress added one in FACTA.

The § 1681b(b)(3)(A) violation has four facts:

| Fact | What you knew in Nov. 2023 | Contestable? |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Agiliti procured a consumer report | Yes. Sterling's Nov. 2 notice said it was reporting public-record information to Agiliti | No |
| (b) Agiliti took adverse action | You knew the start date passed. Whether a decision existed is the open question | **Yes** |
| (c) The action was based in whole or part on the report | Suspicion only. Nobody told you anything | **Yes** |
| (d) You got no copy or summary of rights beforehand | Yes. You knew you received nothing | No |

Your timeliness case rests entirely on (b) and (c). Every discovery allegation should be about **those two facts**: what you could not know, why, and what you learned on what date. "I re-read my emails and realized the law was violated" concedes the point.

**ICRAA (§ 1786.52).** California's discovery rule starts the clock when the plaintiff suspects, or should suspect, injury caused by wrongdoing. *Jolly v. Eli Lilly & Co.*, 44 Cal.3d 1103 (1988) [KNOWN]. A plaintiff relying on delayed discovery pleads (1) the time and manner of discovery and (2) why earlier discovery was not possible despite reasonable diligence. *Fox v. Ethicon Endo-Surgery, Inc.*, 35 Cal.4th 797, 808 (2005) [VERIFIED-REPO; pin unverified]. The house approach from Matter 38 ¶ 24 is the best ICRAA hook: the fact you are suing about is that the denial rested on an *investigative* consumer report. That is precisely what the missing § 1786.40 advice would have told you. Weak spot: Sterling's Nov. 2 notice did name Sterling as the reporting agency.

**The federal pleading posture helps you, so stop pleading against yourself.** Limitations is an affirmative defense. Dismissal is proper only when untimeliness is apparent on the face of the complaint. *Von Saher v. Norton Simon Museum of Art at Pasadena*, 592 F.3d 954, 969 (9th Cir. 2010) [UNVERIFIED in repo]; *Supermail Cargo, Inc. v. United States*, 68 F.3d 1204, 1206–07 (9th Cir. 1995) [UNVERIFIED]. You do not have to anticipate the defense in detail. Once you plead discovery facts, though, the court will judge them. So plead a short, specific, true version, and leave out anything that admits 2023 knowledge.

**Equitable estoppel (draft ¶¶ 55, 57) fails as pleaded.** The Ninth Circuit requires affirmative conduct by the defendant "above and beyond the wrongdoing upon which the plaintiff's claim is filed." *Guerrero v. Gates*, 442 F.3d 697, 706–07 (9th Cir. 2006) [KNOWN]; *Lukovsky v. City & County of San Francisco*, 535 F.3d 1044, 1051–52 (9th Cir. 2008) [KNOWN]. Failing to send the notices is the wrong itself. Fraudulent concealment must also be pleaded with particularity. *Conmar Corp. v. Mitsui & Co. (U.S.A.)*, 858 F.2d 499, 502 (9th Cir. 1988) [UNVERIFIED]. **Keep estoppel only if Agiliti affirmatively told you something**, for example a recruiter saying "your background check is still processing." Otherwise delete ¶ 57 and the estoppel sentence in ¶ 55. A court may read those as a concession that discovery fails on its own.

### § 290.46 (Count Four)
- The basis for the information-and-belief allegations (¶ 42) is thin. *Lerma* lost this count when the report showed court-record sourcing and an NSOR "no record found." Strengthen it with whatever the Oct. 2026 portal showed and the consent form's list of searches. Information-and-belief pleading is allowed where facts are peculiarly in the defendant's possession and the belief rests on factual information that makes the inference plausible. *Soo Park v. - **Defense to expect:** Agiliti's technicians work inside hospitals, so expect a "person at risk" argument under (j)(1) and a search for an "other provision of law" (the *Pastrana* route). ### UCL (Count Five)
- Lost prospective wages are not restitution. *Korea Supply Co. v. Lockheed Martin Corp.*, 29 Cal.4th 1134 (2003) [KNOWN]. In federal court, equitable UCL relief requires an inadequate legal remedy. *Sonner v. Premier Nutrition Corp.*, 971 F.3d 834 (9th Cir. 2020) [KNOWN]. A time-barred legal remedy may still count as "adequate." *Guzman v. Polaris Indus. Inc.*, 49 F.4th 1308 (9th Cir. 2022) [UNVERIFIED]. **The UCL will not rescue time-barred damages.** Reframe it as purely forward-looking injunctive relief, and plead a concrete intent to reapply to Agiliti (or that you have reapplied) to support standing for prospective relief.
