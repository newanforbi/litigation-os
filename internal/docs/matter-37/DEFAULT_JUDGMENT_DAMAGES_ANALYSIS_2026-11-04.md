## 1. Rule 54(c): “according to proof” is not a dollar cap

*AirDoctor, LLC v. Xiamen Qichuang Trade Co., Ltd.*, 134 F.4th 552 (9th Cir. 2025), holds that Rule 54(c) does not bar actual damages on default merely because a complaint seeks damages to be determined at trial without a numerical demand. The panel relied on *Henry v. Sneiders*, 490 F.2d 315 (9th Cir. 1974), and expressly declined to decide whether the plaintiff had **proved** its claimed amount. Thus the more-than-$75,000 amount-in-controversy pleading is a jurisdictional threshold, **not** a ceiling, while the categories pleaded in the prayer remain important. A statute identifying a multiplier provides notice of the kind of additional relief, subject to legal availability and proof.

**Plaintiff argument:** Home Depot was on notice of the accepted-hire wage claim, an expressly identified statutory additional award, exemplary damages and interest. The Rule 55(b)(2) motion quantifies pleaded categories; it does not add new remedies. Distinguish requests for an unpleaded category from a larger amount of an already pleaded category.

**Limit:** The filed complaint, not the internal review draft, controls; the default court independently tests pleading sufficiency, Rule 54(c), and damages proof.

## 2. Cal. Penal Code § 290.46(j)(4)(A): actual PLUS up to 3× additional

The text provides liability for **actual damages, and** a court- or jury-determined amount **not exceeding three times actual damage and not less than $250**, together with identified further remedies. The conjunction “and” supports the maximum **A + 3A = 4A**, where A is proved actual damages; an alternative interpretation limits the combined award to 3A. Argue that the separately enumerated actual and additional elements make 4A the better reading. This is a litigating position, not a verified controlling construction of this particular subsection.

Full additional damages should be grounded in gravity, deliberate employment use of protected-source information, severity of resulting harm and deterrence. **Default is not itself proof of willfulness** and the statute leaves the amount to the court's discretion. The $250 minimum is **not** added again on top of a larger three-times figure. Distinguish the separately mentioned *exemplary damages OR civil penalty up to $25,000*: do not stack both routes in this memo. The filed complaint reportedly does not seek the civil-penalty route.

Source: [Cal. Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E), (j)(4)(A)](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=290.46.&lawCode=PEN).

## 4. Front pay, retirement and emotional distress

A **104-week front-pay** comparator is $575 × 104 = **$59,800** before discount and mitigation. It is a more supportable primary prospective scenario than a lifetime award, but requires evidence of likely comparable tenure, expected schedules, mitigation and duration to replacement employment. Avoid overlap: back pay runs to entry of judgment, prospective loss begins afterward.

The plaintiff will be 67 on **September 16, 2063**; as of a hypothetical April 15, 2028 start for future loss, approximately **35.42 years** remain. At $29,900 real annual wages, undiscounted gross future wages approximate **$1.059 million**. A level annual annuity discounted at 4% real produces approximately **$561,000** present value (rounded; exact convention must be stated). Under an A + 3A model, this future wage component alone could mathematically yield approximately **$2.24 million**. That is an *outer economic illustration*, not an evidence-supported expectation for an at-will job never commenced. An expert would need to support vacancy/tenure probabilities, career trajectory, labor-market replacement, real discounting, and incremental causal effect attributable to **this** denial rather than preexisting registry status.

## 5. Exemplary damages and interest

**Entity-specific financial research:** The [Home Depot U.S.A., Inc. financial-condition evidence dossier](./HOME_DEPOT_USA_FINANCIAL_CONDITION_DOSSIER_2026-11-04.md) documents the SEC consolidated accounts, the named subsidiary's state registry details, corporate rankings and bond/credit evidence. Its controlling finding is that no **standalone Home Depot U.S.A., Inc. financial statement** was located in the public-source pull; group wealth does not itself prove the subsidiary's financial condition or managing-agent ratification. Consult that dossier before proposing punitive proof or corporate asset discovery.\n\n**Exemplary:** § 290.46(j)(4)(A) identifies exemplary relief. For corporate awards, prove Civil Code § 3294(a) oppression, fraud or malice by clear and convincing evidence and § 3294(b) qualifying officer/director/managing-agent conduct or authorization/ratification; *White v. Ultramar, Inc.*, 21 Cal.4th 563, 577 (1999). Establish a meaningful evidentiary basis for the punitive amount and financial condition; *Adams v. Murakami*, 54 Cal.3d 105, 109–10 (1991). A pleaded unlawful registry-use decision and default do **not** establish those independent punitive predicates. An illustrative $250,000 exemplary line belongs only in a **separate conditional scenario** if proved, not in a base request.

**Prejudgment interest:** Cal. Civ. Code § 3288 permits discretionary interest on noncontract damages. Request 7% simple interest on dated past wage losses via a pay-period ledger; do not apply prejudgment interest to future wages or separately multiply interest as actual damages without an articulated legal basis. Recompute on the actual date of judgment and net wage-accrual schedule. Any interest on other damages requires its own rationale. **Postjudgment interest** follows federal **28 U.S.C. § 1961** from entry of judgment, using the statutory Treasury-based rate, daily computation and annual compounding; do not assume a 7% federal postjudgment rate.

## 6. The seven *Eitel* factors

*Eitel v. McCool*, 782 F.2d 1470, 1471–72 (9th Cir. 1986):

1. **Prejudice:** absent judgment, plaintiff cannot redress the alleged statutory hiring injury against a nonappearing defendant.
2. **Merits:** the *properly pleaded* violation and causal adverse decision, if admitted, support substantive liability, subject to the source-specific exception analysis.
3. **Sufficiency:** the accepted hire, screening, § 290.46 source and use, cessation of onboarding and loss identify a plausible claim if the operative complaint contains the requisite facts.
4. **Sum at stake:** favor an auditable wages × time × net-offset model over an unexplained large award; large speculative future/punitive components weaken this factor.
5. **Possible disputes:** default limits disputes over well-pleaded liability, but the judge independently determines damages.
6. **Excusable neglect:** docketed service, notice, failure to respond and absence of an extension would favor plaintiff *if verified*.
7. **Merits preference:** ordinarily cuts against default but is outweighed where defendant declines participation.

Before filing: verify service, the actual Clerk's Rule 55(a) entry, no pending Rule 12 motion/extension, proof of service of any Rule 55(b)(2) application when required, and the district judge's motion procedures.

## 7. Proof-up exhibits and proposed declaration

- **Hire & pay:** Home Depot-specific offer, acceptance, job and site details, rate, expected hours and schedule; authenticated compensation comparators if employer records unavailable.
- **Screen & decision:** screening authorization, CRA identity, *transaction-specific* report and source fields, timestamps, adjudication codes, decision correspondence and compliance exceptions.
- **Back-pay ledger:** each week/pay period; wage, hours, benefits, replacement earnings, other earnings, net loss and supporting paystubs, W-2s and tax records.
- **Employment overlap:** HD Supply dates, exact shifts/hours and whether Home Depot work would have added income; detailed mitigation/applications log.
- **Front-pay proof:** comparable-worker retention, labor-market evidence, reasonable job-search outcomes, discount methodology and no overlapping past/future intervals.
- **Distress proof:** incident-specific chronology, observable effects and available corroboration, distinguishing unrelated sources.
- **Punitive foundation:** employer policy, warnings, decisionmaker/ratification proof and financial-condition evidence; no punitive amount without these.
- **Interest/costs:** date-by-date spreadsheet, statutory rate, receipts, taxable-cost schedule and proposed judgment with distinct nonduplicative lines.

The supporting plaintiff declaration should establish personal knowledge, authenticate each exhibit, describe work opportunities and mitigation truthfully, and attach a clear dated calculation rather than asserting a bottom-line figure.

## 9. Recommended proposed judgment architecture

Request separate findings for (i) actual economic loss net of mitigation; (ii) any independently proved noneconomic actual loss within the pleaded claim; (iii) a discretionary additional amount up to 3× actual damages, with alternative construction preserved; (iv) independently justified exemplary damages **or** statutory civil-penalty route if actually pleaded, not both stacked; (v) prejudgment interest on accrued pecuniary loss; (vi) taxable costs; and (vii) postjudgment interest under 28 U.S.C. § 1961.

**Bottom line:** Favor *AirDoctor* + the statute's **actual-plus-additional** text, but make the provable **net damages base** the centerpiece. The internal valuation memo and `damages.json` are different scenarios; no live matter record, deadline, settlement anchor, or filed pleading is changed by this analysis.
