## Executive finding

Primary sources establish that **Home Depot U.S.A., Inc.** is a separately identified Delaware corporation and that its consolidated parent group, **The Home Depot, Inc.**, has vast sales, earnings, assets, operating cash flow, and investment-grade borrowing access. The publicly reviewed materials **do not disclose an audited standalone balance sheet, standalone net worth, or standalone income statement for the defendant Home Depot U.S.A., Inc.**

**Do not equate group figures with defendant-specific financial condition.** This distinction matters particularly to the conditional **$250,000 exemplary-damages** component in the matter's upper-end damages model. The public record also does **not** establish that an officer/director/managing agent directed or ratified the challenged employment screening decision.

## 7. Relevance to the proposed damages and punitive claim

The connected Matter 37 damages analysis contains a **hypothetical upper model of $1,543,290.01**, including a **$250,000 exemplary-damages component**. The parent group's scale demonstrates commercial context but does **not** independently prove:
1. Home Depot U.S.A., Inc.'s assets **net of liabilities** or other meaningful defendant-level financial condition;
2. Knowing/intentional violation or malice, oppression, or fraud;
3. Authorization or ratification by a corporate officer, director or managing agent; or
4. A permissible dollar amount of exemplary damages.

Under ***Adams v. Murakami***, 54 Cal.3d 105, 109–10, 123 (1991) [S11], the plaintiff bears the burden of providing meaningful defendant-financial-condition evidence to sustain a punitive award. ***Reliant Life Shares, LLC v. Cooper***, 90 Cal.App.5th 14 (2023) [S12], discusses financial-condition proof and non-net-worth measures, but does not transform an unrelated company's consolidated financial statement into the defendant's own.

The defendant's actual role in the employment screen also remains factually unproven to the punitive standard by the mere public identification of executives.

**Do not present this dossier as an SEC-certified standalone valuation, filed court exhibit, or evidence of default.** Existing Matter 37 `clocks.json` records a **November 4, 2026** response deadline. The live ECF docket must confirm any Rule 55(a) default or Rule 55(b)(2) judgment; neither is established here.

## 8. Targeted company-specific evidence requests

**If permitted by the court/procedural posture**, request:

1. **Standalone entity financials:** Home Depot U.S.A., Inc. audited or management-prepared balance sheets, income statements, cash-flow statements, and equity schedules for FY2024/FY2025/latest FY2026 period.
2. **Consolidation bridge:** Subsidiary trial balances and intercompany schedules explaining cash pooling, receivables/payables, dividends, debt allocations and owned property.
3. **Cash, debt, and liabilities:** Actual accounts, liquidity and debt of the named subsidiary, excluding separate affiliates.
4. **Title and capitalization:** Capital-stock/organizational records and proof which assets are owned by the legal defendant.
5. **Relevant decisionmakers:** Corporate screening policies, decision/review records, officers/managing agents authorized to approve employment adjudications, and evidence, if any, of knowledge/ratification.
6. **Authentication:** An informed custodian or appropriately scoped Rule 30(b)(6) representative to explain statements and legal-entity accounting.

**Procedure:** A Rule 55(b)(2) prove-up may involve a court-authorized hearing, accounting or evidence gathering. Disclosure and punitive-financial discovery are **not automatic**, and Cal. Civ. Code § 3295(c) may warrant procedural safeguards [S13]. This is a targeted evidence plan, not an assertion that discovery has already been authorized or served.
