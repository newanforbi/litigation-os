# Matter 37 — Postjudgment collection plan (conditional)

**Case:** *Ngwa Nforbi v. Home Depot U.S.A., Inc.*, E.D. Cal. No. 2:26-cv-03310-TLN-AC  
**Prepared:** November 4, 2026  
**Status:** Internal analysis only. Neither entry of default nor a final money judgment is verified in Matter 37's current GitHub docket records. Nov. 4 remains recorded as the response deadline.

## Objective and sequence

An entered final money judgment makes plaintiff a judgment creditor. It does **not** automatically trigger disbursement from the federal Clerk or direct payment by the defendant.

**Preferred sequence:** verify final judgment → calculate payoff → request voluntary payment → assess Rule 62 stay → if unpaid and executable, use Rule 69 procedures → receive cleared funds → file appropriate satisfaction.

### Step 1 — Verify an actual enforceable money judgment

Confirm the court has entered a **Rule 55(b)(2) default judgment**, not merely the Clerk's Rule 55(a) entry of default or an unsigned proposed judgment. Obtain the judgment entered under Rule 58, determine its exact amount, costs, parties liable and entry date. Check whether all claims and parties are resolved, and whether motions, appeal proceedings or court-ordered stays alter execution.

The debtor identified here must be **Home Depot U.S.A., Inc.**, not automatically its parent or affiliates. Do not infer default or judgment from the November 4, 2026 answer deadline.

### Step 2 — Request voluntary payment promptly

Send the final entered judgment to verified Home Depot counsel or legal-department contact. Provide an itemized payoff statement with judgment principal, allowed costs, section 1961 interest through a specified date, and credit for cleared partial payments. Request a written date and method of payment, along with confirmation of whether any stay or court-approved security exists.

Voluntary payment can be arranged during a stay; no compulsory levy should begin while execution is stayed. Confirm sensitive payment instructions through a secure channel, not in the repository.

### Step 3 — Observe Rule 62

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure **62(a)** ordinarily stays execution and other judgment-enforcement proceedings **30 days from entry** of judgment unless the court orders otherwise. Under **Rule 62(b)**, a longer stay may be obtained through bond or other security **when approved by the court**. A notice of appeal alone does not automatically create an unlimited stay of a money judgment. Check the actual docket for stay orders.

### Step 4 — Execute under Rule 69 if unpaid

**Rule 69(a)(1)** ordinarily enforces federal money judgments by writ of execution. It incorporates California execution procedures to the extent federal law does not otherwise govern. Seek the appropriate writ from the Eastern District of California Clerk, calculate amounts due, identify eligible assets belonging to the named corporate judgment debtor, and follow requirements of the authorized levying officer.

For asset information, **Rule 69(a)(2)** permits postjudgment discovery from the debtor and third parties, through federal or applicable state procedures. California Code of Civil Procedure **§ 708.110** provides a judgment-debtor examination mechanism; **§§ 699.510–699.520** address writ issuance and content. Verify the federal Clerk's form and officer instructions instead of assuming a California state-court form can be filed unchanged.

A second damages lawsuit is ordinarily unnecessary merely to collect an existing enforceable federal judgment.

### Step 5 — Other districts under 28 U.S.C. § 1963

If qualifying corporate assets are located in a different federal district, **28 U.S.C. § 1963** allows registration of a certified judgment after appellate finality or lapse of the appeal period, or earlier with a good-cause order from the rendering court. The registered judgment then has effect in the receiving district. Locate assets of **the actual judgment debtor**, not merely those of a related entity.

### Step 6 — Interest and payment accounting

Under **28 U.S.C. § 1961**, postjudgment interest runs on federal money judgments from the **judgment-entry date** at the weekly average one-year constant-maturity Treasury yield for the preceding calendar week, calculated daily and compounded annually. Do not substitute California's 7% illustrative prejudgment-interest rate.

Maintain a ledger:

| Field | Source |
|---|---|
| Judgment principal and entry date | Final ECF judgment |
| Allowed additional costs | Court order or applicable cost procedure |
| Section 1961 rate | Applicable Treasury yield for week preceding entry |
| Accrued interest | Date-sensitive daily computation with annual compounding |
| Credits and partial payments | Cleared transaction records |
| **Payoff balance** | Unpaid judgment + authorized costs/interest minus credits |

For illustration only, a $454,090 judgment at a hypothetical 4.00% section 1961 rate would generate $18,163.60 over a full simple-reference year before accounting for statutory daily computation, payment events and annual compounding.

### Step 7 — Close upon full satisfaction

Confirm **cleared** full payment before acknowledging satisfaction. Reconcile principal, authorized costs, interest and all credits; discontinue enforcement once paid. File an appropriate acknowledgment in the federal case and address any recorded liens or judgments registered in other districts. California Code of Civil Procedure **§ 724.030** calls for prompt acknowledgment of satisfied money judgments, except where fully satisfied pursuant to a writ, subject to the applicable federal procedural setting.

## Decision checklist

- **Only Clerk's entry of default?** Obtain Rule 55(b)(2) judgment first.
- **Judgment entered, 30-day stay running?** Request voluntary payment; prepare, but do not levy.
- **Appeal filed?** Verify an actual stay and court-approved security.
- **No payment after enforceability?** Writ of execution, targeted Rule 69 asset discovery and authorized levy.
- **Assets only outside the district?** Review § 1963 registration requirements.
- **Full cleared payment?** Record satisfaction and conclude collection.
