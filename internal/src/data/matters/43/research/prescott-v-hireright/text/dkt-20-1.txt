Plaintiff's core premise — that in February 2011 HireRight Solutions had, and
willfully disregarded, an independent pre-adverse action notice obligation to him
under Section 604(b)(3) — is flat out wrong.

The plain text of the FCRA sensibly imposes on the employer, not the CRA, the
duty to provide a pre-adverse action notice to the consumer (i.¢., the job applicant).
The consumer is expecting to hear back from the employer about its ultimate
employment decision (to hire or retain, or not) and may be unaware even of the
CRA’s existence or identity in the employment screening process. For that reason
alone, the purpose animating Section 604(b)(3) — affording the consumer with an
opportunity'to discuss the report with the employer before the employer takes adverse
action — is in no way furthered by requiring pre-adverse action notices from both the
employer and the CRA. To the contrary, a judicial-imposed requirement to provide
such redundant notification could thoroughly confuse the consumer.’

Furthermore, under the U.S. Supreme Court’s landmark opinion in Safeco Ins.
Co. of Am. v. Burr, 551 U.S. 47 (2007), a willful FCRA violation claim fails on the
face of the Complaint where, as here, the defendant’s reading of the FCRA is
supported by the statutory text. Indeed, at all times material to the FAC, all of the
available authorities supported (and, to this day, continue to support) HireRight
Solutions’ reading of Section 604(b)(3). In addition, because under Safeco HireRight
Solutions’ alleged “subjective bad faith” is entirely immaterial, the Court should
dismiss the'FAC with prejudice. In Safeco, the Supreme Court expressly cautioned
against allowing costly and burdensome discovery when, as here, the plaintiff has not

pled facts sufficient to support a willful violation claim.
