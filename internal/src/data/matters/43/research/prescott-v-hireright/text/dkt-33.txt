 1        MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES

 2
 3       I.   INTRODUCTION

 4
 5         In its motion, Defendant HireRight Solutions, Inc. (“HireRight”) argues that
 6    it is not liable under 15 U.S.C. §1681b(b)(3) for providing pre-adverse action
 7   notice because it was not Plaintiff’s employer. HireRight’s arguments fail for the
 8  same reason that Defendant AAA Auto Club of Southern California (“the Auto
 9  Club”)’s argument failed: 1) it is contrary to the expressed words of the statute;
10  and 2) it is contrary to well-established case law that has interpreted the statute.

11
12       II.  LEGAL ANALYSIS

13
14             a. HireRight is Liable Under §1681b(b)(3)

15
16      As more fully set forth in Plaintiff’s opposition to the Auto Club’s motion to
17   dismiss, well-established authority including Goode v. LexisNexis (E.D. Pa. 2012)
18  848 F.Supp.2d 532 holds that “any person who takes adverse action must comply
19  with §1681b(b)(3)(A), be a CRA [consumer reporting agency], an employer, or a
20   staffing agency.” (Id. at 539).
21        HireRight admits in its motion that the court’s ruling in Goode directly
22   contradicts its position that only employers are required to provide pre-adverse
23   action notice. (HireRight’s Mtn to Dismiss, p. 1,3 fn. 12). HireRight also admits
24   that Goode “involved similar factual allegations.” (Id. at p. 18). Nevertheless,
25  HireRight fails to provide any reasoned analysis as to why the Goode holding
26  should not apply in this case. HireRight’s entire attempt at distinguishing Goode is
27  encapsulated in a footnote. (Id. at p. 13, fn. 12).
28        HireRight simply “disagrees” with the court’s opinion because, according to
29  HireRight, it “collapses the distinct obligations imposed on users and CRAs.” (Id.).
30  The problem with HireRight’s argument is that the clear text of the §1681b(b)(3)
31  does not draw any distinction between users and CRAS. As the Goode court states:

 7       The Goode decision is also consistent with the prevailing law in the 9th
 8   Circuit. The 9th Circuit Court in Reynolds held that “‘any person’ who takes an
 9  adverse action is liable.” (Reynolds v. Hartford Financial Services Group, Inc.
10   (9th Cr. 2006) 435 F.3d 1081, rev’d on other grounds sub nom. Safeco Ins. Co. of

13  see also Goode, supra, 848 F.Supp.2d 532 [“…§1681m, a provision of the FCRA
14   that, like §1681b(b)(3)(A), requires anyone taking an adverse action to notify the
15  consumer against whom the action is taken”]). (Emphasis added).
16         Similarly in Adams v. National Engineering Service Corp. (D.Conn. 2009)
17  620 F.Sup.2d 319, the court held a traditional staffing agency to be a consumer
18   reporting agency under the FCRA and stated that “a reasonable jury could also find
19   that [the staffing agency] failed to comply with the notice requirements of section
20  1681b(b)(3) before taking such adverse action.” (620 F.Supp.2d, at 319, 333-332).

23  discusses only when pre-adverse action is required as opposed to who is required
24   to provide pre-adverse action notice. A cursory reading of the Obabueki reveals
25   that no pre-adverse action claim was ever brought against the consumer reporting
26  agency ChoicePoint.2 Moreover, Obabueki is the same case that the Goode court
27   rejected in reaching its holding that pre-adverse action notice is required prior to a

17            b. HireRight Violated §1681b(b)(3) by Not Providing Pre-Adverse
18             Action Notice Prior to Adjudicating Plaintiff as “DOES NOT
19        MEET COMPANY STANDARDS”

20
21      As more fully discussed in Plaintiff’s Opposition to the Auto Club’s Motion
22   to Dismiss Plaintiff’s SAC, the Goode case clearly holds that pre-adverse action
23   notice is required prior to the consumer reporting agency’s negative adjudication of
24  a consumer’s eligibility for employment. (848 F.Supp.2d at 540).
25       However, this does not mean that multiple notices are required. One notice
26   is sufficient so long as the notice furthers FCRA’s objectives. (Reynolds, supra,
27  435 F.3d at 1096). In Goode, the court held that whenever a consumer reporting
28  agency automatically renders adjudication on a consumer’s eligibility for
29  employment based on criteria supplied by a third party, pre-adverse action notice
30  was required before the adjudication. (Goode, supra, 848 F.Supp.2d at 539-540.)
31  This is because, the “adjudication of plaintiffs is, quite literally, a decision for

 3        HireRight argues that requiring consumer reporting agencies to provide pre-
 4  adverse action notices prior to their adjudication would impose impractical

16              c.  Plaintiff Does Not Oppose HireRight’s Motion to Dismiss
17                Plaintiff’s Claims Related to Willful Violation of §1681b(b)(3)

18
19         In Goode, the Court held that what constitutes and adverse action is not
20  always clear. (848 F.Supp.2d at 543). While it found that a consumer reporting
21  agency’s adjudication as “noncompetitive” to be adverse action, it stated that it was
22  a “close case” given that no court of appeals had taken on this issue. (Id.).
23  Accordingly, Plaintiff respects the Goode court’s opinion and does not oppose
24  HireRight’s motion to dismiss Plaintiff’s claims on the issue of willfulness.
25   Plaintiff requests only to be allowed leave to amend upon receipt of evidence that
26  HireRight continued to fail to provide pre-adverse action notice following Goode.
27       However, just because Plaintiff does not seek a willful claim for HireRight’s
28   violation of the §1681b(b)(3) does not mean that the Auto Club cannot not be held
29   liable for willful violation of the same statute. The Goode court did not find any
30  ambiguity in the statutory text that parties other than an employer may be required
31   to provide pre-adverse action notice:

14            d. Plaintiff Has Stated Sufficient Facts To State A Claim Under 15
15             U.S.C. §1681e(a)(1)

16
17       15 U.S.C. §1681e states that:
18             “Every consumer reporting agency shall maintain reasonable
19              procedures designed to…limit the furnishing of consumer reports to
20               the purposes listed under section 1681b of this title. These procedures
21                shall require that prospective users of the information identify
22              themselves, certify purposes for which the information is sought, and
23                 certify that the information will be used for no other purpose. Every
24             consumer reporting agency shall make a reasonable effort to verify the
25                identity of a new prospective user and the uses certified by such
26               prospective user prior to furnishing such user a consumer report. No
27             consumer reporting agency may furnish a consumer report to any
28              person if it has reasonable grounds for believing that the consumer
29               report will not be used for a purpose listed in section 1681b of this
30                   title.”
31        HireRight argues that it complied with this statute’s requirements by
32  obtaining certification from both Advanced Towing and the Auto Club. However,

 1  other than simply receiving their certifications, HireRight does not specify what
 2  other procedures were utilized to ascertain the users’ permissible purpose.
 3         In Pintos v. Pacific Creditor’s Ass’n (9th Cir. 2010) 605 F.3d 665, the 9th
 4   Circuit held that merely obtaining certifications from prospective users are
 5   insufficient. (Id. at 677). The court states:
 6               “After prospective subscribers ‘certify the purposes for which credit
 7              information is sought, and certify that the information will be used for
 8            no other purpose,’ the reporting agency must make ‘reasonable effort’
 9                to verify the certifications and may not furnish reports if ‘reasonable
10              grounds’ exist to believe that reports will be used impermissibly.
11            Under the plain terms of §1681e(a), a subscriber’s certification cannot
12              absolve the reporting agency of its independent obligations to verify
13               the certification and determine that no reasonable grounds exist for
14               suspecting impermissible use. Blanket certification cannot eliminate
15              genuine issues of material fact with regard to [the consumer reporting
16              agency’s] liability.” (Id.).
17        Accordingly, if this court finds that existing law mandates that Plaintiff’s
18  consumer report was not used for employment purposes then HireRight violated
19   this statute by not consulting the appropriate legal authority from which it could
20  conclude that the Auto Club was not authorized to receive the report.
21      As Plaintiff alleged in his SAC, HireRight furnished consumer report
22  information to the Auto Club by providing the Auto Club with the results of
23   Plaintiff’s negative adjudication. The Auto Club obtained this information and in
24   fact used this information to recommend Plaintiff’s termination through its “denial
25  of access” notification. (See Adams, supra, 620 F.Supp.2d at 325 [a
26  communication telling the ultimate employer that the applicant is not eligible for
27   the position constitutes a consumer report]). Thus, by providing such information
28   to the Auto Club, the law requires HireRight to take reasonable efforts to verify
29   that the use was permissible. Accordingly, if the Auto Club’s purpose was found to
30  be impermissible based on existing case, law, §1681e mandates that HireRight
31  should have consulted the same prior to furnishing Plaintiff’s consumer report
32  information.
