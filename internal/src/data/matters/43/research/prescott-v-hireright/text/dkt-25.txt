 2    PROCEDURAL REQUIREMENTS FOR TAKING ADVERSE ACTION AGAINST
 3                         CONSUMERS

 4
 5       51. Under the FCRA, 15 U.S.C. §1681b(b)(3)(A), a user or joint user of background check
 6   reports or consumer reports, must provide to the job applicant or employee a notice containing
 7   the following information, before taking any adverse employment action:

 8                a. A copy of the consumer report
 9               b. A description in writing of the rights of the consumer under the FCRA; and
10                 c. A reasonable opportunity to dispute the information before rendering the adverse
11              employment decision.
12       52. After an adverse action occurs, the consumer job applicant or employee must receive a
13   second notice, mandated by 15 U.S.C. §1681m(a) informing the applicant or employee that
14   adverse employment action has been taken. (Hereafter, Plaintiff will refer to the first of those
15   notices as the “pre-adverse action notice” and the second as the “adverse action notice.”)
16       53. The reasons for the pre-adverse action notice requirement in employment situations are to
17   alert the job applicant that he or she is about to be rejected based on the content of a report, and
18   to give him an opportunity to challenge the accuracy of the information with the credit reporting
19   agency or the employer before the job prospect is lost.

20       54. Congress recognizes that these rights are extremely important to the consumer and help
21   “to insure that consumer reporting agencies exercise their grave responsibilities with fairness,
22   impartiality, and a respect for the consumer’s right to privacy.” (15 U.S.C. §1681(a)(4)).
23   (Emphasis added).

24       55. Even where the applicant was justly denied employment, he would be informed that his
25   criminal history was a factor in the employer’s adverse employment decision, and would be
26   motivated to seek various state remedies to expunge his criminal records. In California, this is
27   called a dismissal pursuant to Penal Code §1203.4.
28       56. To ensure knowing compliance with the FCRA, Congress further requires that before any
29  consumer reporting agency may provide consumer reports on an applicant, the reporting agency
30  must have obtained a certification from the employer that it will comply with 15 U.S.C.
31   §1681b(b)(3) by providing the consumer with a pre-adverse action notice whenever the employer
