# Opposing counsel attack — Verified Complaint, Ngwa Nforbi v. The Hertz Corporation

Run November 12, 2026 against the Santa Clara Superior Court version (23 pp.), from the chair of Hertz's counsel preparing a Rule 12(b)(6) motion after a federal filing. One-sided by design. This is working material, not an assessment of the case. Fixes applied in the federal rebuild are listed at the end.

## 1. Core theory of attack

This case stands or falls on one inference: that someone at Hertz used the registry entry rather than the DMV entry the Notice actually names. The complaint pleads the obvious alternative explanation itself. It concedes a valid "alcohol" basis exists in the MVR and a felony conviction flagged for the same review, so the registry theory is merely consistent with liability, not plausible (*Iqbal*, 556 U.S. at 678–82 [V-general]). Take that inference away and Counts 1 and 3 become each other's alternatives with no factual anchor, and the remaining counts are paperwork claims with no injury.

## 2. Reconstructed argument (stripped)

1. Hertz bought a report containing a registry hit, a conviction, and DMV refusal actions.
2. The vendor graded the clean registry result "Meets" and the hits "Client Review Required."
3. Hertz's notice cited "alcohol."
4. *Therefore* Hertz used the registry entry.

Step 4 does not follow. In the same report, every clean court search is also graded "Meets Company Standards." "Meets" for a null result proves only that nothing was found, not that registry status is a hiring criterion. "Client Review Required" proves a human was told to look. Looking is not using, and *Donaleski* [V-repo] says awareness does not taint a decision resting on lawful sources.

## 3. Primary lines of attack

- **"Use" rests on a grading convention.** Stated above. Paragraphs 34–35 overread "Meets Company Standards."
- **Source: NSOPW is a federal disclosure.** NSOPW is a federal website created by 34 U.S.C. § 20922. Information obtained there is "disclosed pursuant to" federal law, not § 290.46. The complaint never alleges that HireRight or Hertz visited meganslaw.ca.gov.
- **Ripeness and injury.** No final decision exists, Plaintiff never responded to the Notice, and his own paragraphs say the decision is "preliminary." Damages from a preliminary notice that he ignored are speculative, which raises an Article III problem for every count.
- **ICRAA standing.** This is a bare format violation. Under *TransUnion*, 594 U.S. 413 [V-general], a procedural disclosure defect without concrete harm is not an injury in fact. Plaintiff alleges no confusion and no consequence of the format. Separately, Hertz provided a clean standalone FCRA form, and page 8 was a separate screen in the e-sign flow; the PDF is only an archival compilation.
- **Labor Code § 432.7.** A DMV suspension record is a licensing record, not a "record of arrest." Plaintiff supplies the arrest from his own memory; the record Hertz saw shows no arrest. The job was "Driver Required," and reviewing a driving record for a driving job is lawful.
- **Non-compete pleaded "on information and belief."** The agreement sits in Plaintiff's own portal. Information-and-belief pleading is improper for facts within the plaintiff's control. And Hertz has never attempted to enforce anything, so there is no actual damage and no Article III injury.
- **Pattern-or-practice injunction.** One applicant's file does not support a "pattern" allegation; that allegation is conclusory.

## 4. If I were the judge

I would not accept that a vendor's grading label is Hertz's hiring rule without some fact showing that Hertz chose the registry product or acted on the result. I would ask Plaintiff: "Where in this record does anyone at Hertz look at the registry entry, rather than the DMV entry the Notice names?" I would also note that the complaint says no "person at risk" was identified, which is a conclusion. The job is customer-facing, and Plaintiff's own exhibit shows a protective order. Finally, I would want to know why Plaintiff did not respond to a notice inviting him to do so.

## 5. Surgical strikes

1. "Every clean search in this report says 'Meets Company Standards.' By Plaintiff's logic, Hertz 'uses' the Wyoming federal docket too."
2. "The Notice names the DMV record. Plaintiff pleads the DMV record. He then asks the Court to assume a different reason."
3. "NSOPW is a federal website. Section 290.46 governs California's website."
4. "Plaintiff never answered the Notice, and now sues over the decision he was invited to change."
5. "He pleads the content of a document he can open himself 'on information and belief.'"

## 6. What it hides

- Plaintiff's offenses (forcible sexual penetration; assault with intent to commit rape) and an active protective order. These matter to "person at risk."
- The job is customer-facing.
- Plaintiff's other § 290.46 suits (UPS, FedEx, Randstad, Amazon, Home Depot). Defense counsel will use them to paint him as a serial litigant.
- His non-response to the Notice.
- That the registry product was disclosed to him on a California form he received.

## Fixes applied in the federal rebuild

| Attack | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Meets" overread | Recast ¶¶ on company standards. The point is now that Hertz **selected** a separately itemized registry product for a California job and **routed** registry hits into its eligibility review, followed by a decision. Concedes that null results grade "Meets." |
| NSOPW = federal disclosure | Added: NSOPW holds no registry data of its own. It displays California's § 290.46 website records in real time, so California remains the discloser and NSOPW is only the conduit. |
| Ripeness / non-response | Added: the preliminary decision is itself a use. Hertz's own five-day hold expired Nov. 9. Plaintiff is ready, willing, and able to start, and Hertz has excluded him from the job. |
| ICRAA standing | Added concrete harm: the only signed document listed **no** products. The registry search appeared only on the unsigned California page, so Plaintiff authorized it without the disclosure, and the result was used against him. |
| Non-compete on I&B | Removed the I&B allegation about the agreement's contents. The count now rests on the offer letter's own term making a non-competition agreement a condition of employment. Article III risk noted below. |
| Person at risk | Added: the exception requires use *to protect* an identified person. Hertz screens categorically, and the Notice identifies no one. |
| § 432.7 arrest | Added Veh. Code §§ 23612(a)(1)(A) and 13353: the refusal action exists only after a lawful arrest, so the DMV entry itself records that arrest [V-general; confirm text]. |
| Federal question | Added a federal FCRA § 1681e(b) count against HireRight (see the risk note below). |

**Remaining risk after the fixes:**
- Count 1 "use" is still contestable but now pleaded on Hertz's own choices.
- The FCRA count depends on attributing the "[Criminal Information]" label to HireRight's adjudication system rather than to Hertz's manual review.
- ICRAA and non-compete standing remain the most likely dismissals in federal court. A standing dismissal is without prejudice and can be refiled in state court.
