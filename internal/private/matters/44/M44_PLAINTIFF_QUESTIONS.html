How to use this. Answer only what is true, in your own words. A "No" or "I don't remember" is fine and useful. Why accuracy matters more than helpfulness here. You will be asked these same questions under oath at deposition. One case, Olea (N.D. Cal. 2023), lost standing because the plaintiff's deposition showed he understood the form. A true "no" now is far better than a "yes" that falls apart later.
