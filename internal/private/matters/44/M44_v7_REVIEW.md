## New facts in v7 (adopted into the record)
- **Employer identified (answers Q C1):** pay statements dated Oct. 16 and Oct. 30, 2026 name "HOTEL MANAGEMENT GROUP CALIFORNIA, INC. (27329) d/b/a DELTA SANTA CLARA." The header is Oasis Outsourcing III, LLC, and the checks are drawn on Oasis Outsourcing, LLC (PEO).
- **Pay rate:** the regular rate is **$24/hr** on both statements, including the Oct. 18–24 front-desk week. This supports Plaintiff's $24-for-both-roles recollection over the offer's $23 GSA rate.
- **Actual paid hours:** 51.43 h / $1,233.36 (Sept. 27–Oct. 10); **71.36 h / $1,696.08 (Oct. 11–24)**, both including overtime. The Oasis portal shows 80.00 standard hours per pay period (not a guarantee).
- **Revised baseline (actual paid hours, Oct. 11–24):** about 35.7 h/wk, about $848/wk. Lost wages, Nov. 1–14, against that baseline: (35.7 − 22.5) + (35.7 − 15) ≈ 33.9 h × $24 ≈ **$813**. **Defensible range now $533–$813**, depending on whether the baseline is the scheduled hours or the actual paid hours. Overtime in Oct. 11–24 means actual hours exceeded scheduled hours. Expect the defense to argue that week was training-heavy.
- **Harrell Hospitality Group, LLC (Texas):** a Marriott job ad (No. 26087200) says applicants will be employed by "franchisee Harrell Hospitality," which "controls hiring, firing, discipline, staffing, and pay." Harrell shares HMG's Dallas registered office. Its 2003 10-KSB says HMG-California held the Santa Clara management contract (and changed its name in 2000).
- **Ownership:** the 2025 Delta FDD, Ex. M, lists **Kang Family Partners, L.P.** as franchisee of record. A 2022 release says the hotel is owned by Kang Family Partners, **LLC** and managed by Harrell. FDD form agreement § 8.2: employment decisions are made solely by the franchisee or management company.
- ***Century Indemnity Co. v. Hearrean* (2002) 98 Cal.App.4th 734:** exists. It is a coverage case over Kang Family Partners' construction-defect suit at the hotel. Cited in v7 only for chronology. Fine as a source for an information-and-belief allegation, but unnecessary in a complaint.

## Ranked fixes
1. **Drop Marriott. Move Kang to Doe status, or drop it. Keep HMG and Harrell.**
   - v7 itself pleads FDD § 8.2, which allocates all employment decisions to the franchisee or manager.
   - Plaintiff's alleged reliance (accepting the job; signing the authorization) does not connect to the injury, which is the *missing disclosure* by HMG under HMG's own trade name.
   - *Patterson v. Domino's* (2014) 60 Cal.4th 474, 497–98 requires control over the relevant conduct.
   - An FCRA apparent-authority theory exists in some circuits (e.g., *Jones v. Federated Fin. Reserve Corp.*, 144 F.3d 961 (6th Cir. 1998) [verify]), but it will not survive against a franchisor on these facts.
   - Marriott brings the best-funded motion to dismiss, at a cost of months.
   - Kang: the deed is unconfirmed, the LP/LLC identity is unresolved, and owner liability for a manager's screening is thin. Keep "Kang Family Partners, L.P. and LLC" as Does (v7 ¶ 7 already reserves them).
   - **Harrell is worth keeping:** the job ad's "controls hiring, firing, discipline, staffing, and pay" is a real joint-employer fact.
2. **Count IV: hold it, or replace the conditional pleading with facts.**
   - Delete "Plaintiff's theory is" (¶ 57) and the "If Howell used…" structure (¶ 58).
   - Delete ¶ 31's concession of alternative sources and ¶ 25's ankle-monitor sentence.
   - Plead Count IV only when the UBS file (or a witness) supplies a registry source (complaint draft ¶ 25 / Alternative 24-B).
   - Not pleading the defense's alternative-source theory is proper. Pleading provenance without a basis is not, so if there is no basis, Count IV waits.
   - The conservative deadline is Oct. 13, 2027.
3. **Strengthen ¶ 21 (standing):**
   - Add "never received any disclosure, through any channel" (*TransUnion*, 594 U.S. at 440–42).
   - Add "would not have signed an authorization covering his entire employment 'without reservation'" (*Syed* at 499–500), **if true (Q A5)**.
   - Add the point that the injury does not depend on inaccuracy, because Megan's Law information may not be used for employment at all (Pen. Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E)).
   - **Delete** "correct or explain any inaccurate or misleading information." Plaintiff alleges no inaccuracy, and the phrase invites *Gray* and *Williams v. DISA*.
4. **Cut ¶¶ 2, 4–5 to essentials.**
   - Remove the repeated "Plaintiff does not allege…" disclaimers. Keep one sentence per theory limiting it.
   - Remove the OpenCorporates and assessor-roll narrative and the *Century Indemnity* chronology.
   - Target length: 15–17 pp. Rule 8(a)(2) asks for a short and plain statement.
5. **Damages (¶ 33):**
   - Plead the actual-paid-hours baseline from the pay statements and a computed figure ("approximately $533–$813 through November 14, accruing").
   - Replace the training distress, which the violations did not cause, with the housing-insecurity distress tied to the shift cut. Use the rent comparison and the job search from the Nov. 12 draft ¶ 24.
   - Do not request attorney's fees while pro se. It is harmless but signals overreach.
6. **Replace "share-copy packet" (¶¶ 15, 18, 52)** with the plain fact: "The packet attached to the September 22 email consisted of three pages: …". "Share copy" is an artifact of the review binder.
7. **Count II:** keep it as an alternative only after the UBS file. Otherwise hold it with Count IV (both rest on the same source fact).
8. **Consistency:** use "Causes of Action" throughout (¶ 8 says "Counts I and II"). Redate at filing (v7 is dated Nov. 13). Confirm the caption name per Plaintiff's preference.

**How Marriott is kept (two independent theories):**
1. **Direct "cause to be procured" (¶¶ 15–17).**
   - Facts from Gmail: the Sept. 27, 2026 Marriott Systems Support email ("Your Marriott Enterprise ID has been created"; "contains information from Marriott International, Inc."). It was sent to HR (Venegas) and Plaintiff five days after the signed authorization, with the password built from Plaintiff's name and DOB. That shows Marriott received the same identifiers collected for the CRA.
   - Also: Power of M training (roster Oct. 22; Plaintiff's Oct. 19 access request text), the MARSHA code, Marriott MFA, and "Marriott/Shadow" roster labels.
   - On information and belief, Marriott conditions EID and system access on completed screening. The report was procured at least partly to satisfy that requirement, so Marriott "caused" procurement under § 1681b(b)(2)(A) and § 1786.16.
   - Supported by *Soo Park* and identified under Rule 11(b)(3).
   - **Weakest link:** no document yet shows a Marriott screening requirement. Discovery target: Marriott brand standards and EID-issuance criteria.
2. **Ostensible agency (¶¶ 18–19; Civ. Code §§ 2300, 2317, 2334).**
   - Holding out: the Marriott logo and "Delta Hotels by Marriott" welcome on the offer; HR signature; a Marriott job ad; an EID email in Marriott International's name; Marriott training systems.
   - Reliance is tied to the injury: Plaintiff signed the Authorization and did not question the missing disclosures because he believed it was a Marriott process (**Question M2: confirm true**).
   - Pre-empts the franchise agreement § 8.2 defense: an undisclosed private allocation does not defeat ostensible authority created by the principal's own manifestations.

**Expected defense:** a Rule 12(b)(6) motion by Marriott (*Patterson*-style franchisor arguments; no allegation that Marriott ordered the report; reliance not causally tied). Count on that motion being filed and argued. HMG and Harrell are the core defendants either way.

**New questions for Plaintiff:**
- **M1.** Do you have any basis, beyond the EID timing, to believe Marriott required or reviewed background screening? For example, anything said at onboarding about clearance before system access, an Identity Shoppe or EID screen mentioning screening, or a training module on it. If none, ¶ 17 rests on timing plus information and belief. That is permissible under Rule 11(b)(3) but thin.
- **M2.** Is it true that you believed you were being hired and screened through a Marriott process, and that because of that belief you signed without asking about the missing disclosure documents? If not, delete ¶ 19's bracketed sentence; ostensible agency then fails for lack of reliance.
