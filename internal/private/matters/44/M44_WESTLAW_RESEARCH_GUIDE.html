3. Questions, search strings, and what to look for

Q1. Article III standing for § 1681b(b)(2)(A) after TransUnion MUST → complaint ¶ 13, Counts One and Three

adv: "1681b(b)(2)" /p (standing "article III" "concrete injury" "concrete harm") /p TransUnion & DA(aft 06/24/2021) adv: "1681b(b)(2)" /p (privacy intru! seclusion) /p (standing concrete) & DA(aft 06/24/2021) adv: "stand-alone" "standalone" "consist! solely" /s disclosure /p (standing "article III") & DA(aft 06/24/2021)

What to look for:

  • Courts that accepted privacy intrusion (a report obtained without informed consent) as a harm close to the traditional common-law kind. Compare those courts that called it a bare procedural / informational violation. Write down what facts the plaintiff alleged in each.
  • Whether the Ninth Circuit has revisited Syed v. M-I, LLC, 853 F.3d 492 (9th Cir. 2017) after TransUnion. Run KeyCite on Syed and filter Citing References: Cases, Ninth Circuit, after June 2021, and the "standing" headnote.
  • Cases where the plaintiff alleged "I would not have signed" or "I did not understand what I was authorizing", and whether that was enough.
  • Dismissed versus remanded: in removed cases, a lack of standing leads to remand to state court. In cases filed in federal court, it leads to dismissal without prejudice. Mark which outcome each case had.

Q2. A disclosure that was never given at all (not merely cluttered) MUST → ¶¶ 9–10, 28

adv: "1681b(b)(2)" /p (never no fail! omit!) /s (provid! receiv! gave) /s disclosure adv: "1681b(b)(2)" /p (acknowledg! certif!) /s receiv! /s disclosure

Why it matters: most reported cases involve extraneous language in the disclosure (e.g., Gilberg v. California Check Cashing Stores, 913 F.3d 1169 (9th Cir. 2019)). Our facts are different: the disclosure is missing entirely, and the form says it was "received." Look for cases where no disclosure was given, and see whether courts treat that as a clearer violation (helpful on willfulness) and a more concrete harm (helpful on standing).

Q3. Defects in the authorization SHOULD → ¶ 29

adv: "1681b(b)(2)(A)(ii)" authoriz! /p (release waiver "without reservation" acknowledg!)
  • Can the authorization be on the same page as an acknowledgment and a third-party release? What did Syed hold about liability waivers? Is the authorization invalid when it rests on disclosures that were never given? If no case supports it, drop ¶ 29.

Q4. § 1681b(b)(3): cutting a current employee's shifts as "adverse action" MUST → Count Two

adv: "1681b(b)(3)" /p ("adverse action" "1681a(k)") /p (hours shift! schedul! reduc! demot!) adv: "1681a(k)(1)(B)(ii)" /p (current existing) /3 employee adv: "1681b(b)(3)" /p (standing concrete) /p ("opportunity to" dispute respond explain) & DA(aft 06/24/2021)
  • Is a reduction in hours (not a firing) an "adverse action"? Look for cases involving current employees, not just applicants.
  • What counts as "based in whole or in part" on the report, and how much detail plaintiffs had to plead about the report-to-decision link.
  • Walker v. Fred Meyer, Inc., 953 F.3d 1082 (9th Cir. 2020): read its (b)(3) holding carefully (KeyCite it). Write down exactly what it says about whether (b)(3) requires an actual adverse action. [My characterization of Walker has not been verified.]

Q5. Willfulness (needed for statutory damages) SHOULD → ¶ 30

adv: "1681b(b)(2)" /p (willful! reckless!) /p Safeco /p (template form vendor "objectively unreasonable")
  • Did Syed hold that the standalone requirement is "unambiguous," which would make a violation reckless? Can an employer avoid willfulness by relying on a vendor's or SHRM's form? (Our packet's policy page is a SHRM template, V05 p.26.)

Q6. ICRAA (Civ. Code § 1786.16) in federal court MUST → Count Three

adv: "1786.16" /p (disclosure "investigative consumer report") /p (employ! employer) adv: ("1786.16" ICRAA "investigative consumer reporting agencies act") /p (standing "article III") & DA(aft 06/24/2021) adv: ICRAA /p (preempt! "1681t") adv: "1786.50" /p ("ten thousand" "$10,000") /p (per violation each greater)
  • Federal standing for ICRAA: Parsonage applies only in state court. Find federal cases deciding whether an ICRAA form violation alone is a concrete injury.
  • Is the $10,000 owed per violation or per plaintiff? Do the disclosure defect and the missing check-box count as separate violations?
  • Do courts treat a criminal background check as an "investigative consumer report" (Connor v. First Student (2018) 5 Cal.5th 1026)? Is there any overlap or "election" defense between ICRAA and CCRAA?
  • Can a user (the employer) be liable under § 1786.50, or only the agency?
  • Parsonage v. Wal-Mart Assocs., 118 Cal.App.5th 399 (2026): KeyCite it first. Has the California Supreme Court granted review or depublished it? (Cal. Rules of Court 8.1115(e).) Also check the conflicting Limon v. Circle K Stores Inc. (2022) 84 Cal.App.5th 671 [verify citation].

Q7. The signed acknowledgment of "receipt" SHOULD

adv: (FCRA "1681b" ICRAA) /p (acknowledg! certif!) /s (receiv! "read and understand") /p (estop! waiv! bar!)
  • Will the employer argue the signature estops the plaintiff or waives the claim? Look for any case holding that FCRA rights cannot be waived by a boilerplate acknowledgment.

Q8. Supplemental jurisdiction and tolling SHOULD → memo § 2.6

adv: "1367(d)" /p (toll!) /p (standing "lack of jurisdiction" "subject matter") /p (federal claim) adv: "1367(c)" /p ("290.46" "novel" "complex issue of state law") /p (FCRA "1681")
  • Does § 1367(d) stop the clock when the federal claim is dismissed for lack of standing, as opposed to when the court declines supplemental jurisdiction? (Start from Artis v. District of Columbia, 583 U.S. 71 (2018), Citing References.) This decides how fast we must refile in state court if federal court dismisses.

Q9. Penal Code § 290.46: updates since September 2026 SHOULD

adv: "290.46" /p (employ! "person at risk" user) adv: "290.46" /p ("340(a)" "338(a)" limitation! penalty) adv: ("statute for a penalty" "340(a)") /p ("civil penalty" "exemplary") /p (individual aggrieved) & DA(aft 01/01/2015)
  • Any new § 290.46 employment ruling, especially on who counts as a "user" (does it reach a non-decisionmaking coworker or trainer?) and on "person at risk." Compare the cases already logged: Mendoza, Donaleski, Pastrana, Lerma.
  • Does the one-year limit in CCP § 340(a) apply to the § 290.46 civil-penalty part? If so, the binding filing date is Oct. 13, 2027.
  • Can an employer be held vicariously liable under a statute that imposes liability on "the user"?

7. Red flags: stop and note them when you see them

  • Red flag / "superseded" / "abrogated by TransUnion" on any standing case decided before 2021.
  • Ninth Circuit memorandum dispositions can be cited if issued on or after Jan. 1, 2007, but they are not precedent (9th Cir. R. 36-3). District court opinions bind no one; mark them "persuasive."
  • California opinions under review: once review is granted, a published opinion may be cited only as CRC 8.1115(e) allows. Write down the date of the grant.
  • Cases where the employer did give a disclosure and the dispute was only about its format. These are distinguishable from ours; note the difference.
  • Cases that hurt us: write them down too. The handoff requires listing unfavorable authority.
