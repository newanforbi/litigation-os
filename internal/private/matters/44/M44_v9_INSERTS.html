2. Two factual problems to fix before filing

A. The 70% text came after the first cut was posted, not "two days before" (¶ 42). ¶ 38 says the Nov. 1–7 roster was posted and photographed no later than 7:03 a.m. on Oct. 30. Your 70% progress text to Barros was sent at 10:55 a.m. on Oct. 30, about four hours later. As written, ¶ 42 says that two days before the roster was posted you had reported 70%. The timestamps contradict that, and defense counsel would use it against your credibility.

Replace in ¶ 42

Two days before, Plaintiff had reported to Barros that he could perform about 70% of Night Audit tasks without help.
The cut was already posted when, later that same morning, Plaintiff reported to Barros that he could perform about 70% of Night Audit tasks without Howell's help. Barros made no change to the roster.

Corrected, this fact actually helps you. The first cut was decided before Barros had your own progress report, so the only overnight information he could have had came through Howell (¶ 24A).

B. "Sole authority" (¶ 24) does not fit the rosters as written. Night Auditor Thao Truong was also scheduled on overnights with you on Oct. 11, Oct. 12, and Oct. 26, and on Nov. 2, the night you called out. The defense will put the rosters in front of the court. If Thao worked with you but did not evaluate or report on you, say so. If Thao did, "sole" has to go.

Replace the last sentence of ¶ 24

Night Auditor Thao Truong also worked the overnights of October 11, 12, and 26 with Plaintiff, [but did not evaluate Plaintiff's training or report on it to Barros]. Based on Plaintiff's personal knowledge of the training structure, Howell was the trainer responsible for assessing and evaluating Plaintiff's Night Audit performance and for reporting that assessment to Barros, the manager who set Plaintiff's schedule.

New question T1: Did Thao train, evaluate, or report on you? Who told you Howell was your trainer?

3. The Howell–Barros emails

You're right that they are probably the center of the case. ¶ 32 already pleads them correctly, on information and belief, with ¶ 33's basis. Three things follow from that.

IssueWhat to do
What you know vs. what you believeIf you have seen any of these messages, or someone told you about one, that is a far stronger basis. Add it to ¶ 33 as a new (j), stating what, when, and from whom. Even partial knowledge counts ("a coworker told me Howell emails John about trainees after each shift"). If your belief comes only from the facts already in ¶ 33, leave ¶ 32 as is. Don't describe the contents of messages you haven't seen.
Keep them from disappearingSend the preservation letter (separate PDF) before filing, and soon. Company email can be purged on a retention schedule. Howell's personal phone texts can be deleted. Front-desk video is usually overwritten within weeks, and the Oct. 29 morning is already about 19 days old. Once the letter is received, any later loss of these records can be sanctioned (Fed. R. Civ. P. 37(e)).
Don't get them yourselfDon't access Hotel email, Howell's accounts, or Hotel computers to retrieve or photograph these messages. Doing so could expose you to claims under Cal. Penal Code § 502 and the federal Computer Fraud and Abuse Act, and it could hand the defense a way to attack the evidence. Get them through discovery: Rule 34 requests to HMG and Harrell, a Rule 45 subpoena for Howell's personal-device texts if needed, and early-discovery protocols if this District's General Order 71 applies [verify].
First discovery request"All communications (email, text, iMessage, Teams, or other) between Justin Howell and John Barros, or between either of them and any other Hotel employee, referring to Plaintiff, from October 1 through December 31, 2026, including on personal devices used for work." Serve it right after the Rule 26(f) conference.
