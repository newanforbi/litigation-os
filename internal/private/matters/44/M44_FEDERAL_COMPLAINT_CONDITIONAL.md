### First Cause of Action — Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(2)(A) (against Employer)

26. Plaintiff incorporates ¶¶ 1–23.

27. Employer procured or caused to be procured a consumer report about Plaintiff for employment purposes without first providing a clear and conspicuous written disclosure, in a document consisting solely of the disclosure, that a consumer report may be obtained for employment purposes (¶¶ 8–12).

28. Employer acted willfully, or at minimum recklessly. Its own Acknowledgment recites the existence of separate FCRA disclosure documents, which shows awareness of the requirement, yet it transmitted a packet that did not contain them. *Safeco Ins. Co. of Am. v. Burr*, 551 U.S. 47, 57 (2007). [Alternatively, negligently, 15 U.S.C. § 1681o.]

29. Plaintiff suffered concrete injury as alleged in ¶ 13 and is entitled to statutory damages of not less than $100 and not more than $1,000, punitive damages, and costs under 15 U.S.C. § 1681n(a). In the alternative, he is entitled to actual damages under § 1681o.

### Second Cause of Action — ICRAA, Civil Code § 1786.16 (against Employer)

30. Plaintiff incorporates ¶¶ 1–13.

31. Employer caused an investigative consumer report about Plaintiff to be prepared or procured for employment purposes without first providing the clear and conspicuous standalone written disclosure required by Civil Code § 1786.16(a)(2)(B) [verify subdivision]. That disclosure must identify the investigative consumer reporting agency, the nature and scope of the investigation, the permissible purpose, and the agency's website. Employer also failed to provide a means by which Plaintiff could check a box to receive a copy of the report (§ 1786.16(b)(1)) [verify].

32. Plaintiff is entitled to his actual damages or $10,000, whichever is greater, and costs under Civil Code § 1786.50(a). If the violation was grossly negligent or willful, he is entitled to punitive damages under § 1786.50(b).
