## 1. One-page recommendation

**Bottom line: do not file the § 290.46 complaint now, in either court.** The current draft cannot answer the question that decides the case: where Howell's information came from. On this record the Oct. 29 remark (*"He released you didn't he?"*) is fully explained by the visible ankle monitor, without any registry lookup. Meanwhile the decisionmaker (Barros) has three written, contemporaneous attendance reasons (T-06, T-07, T-08). *Lerma* (source attribution decided at the pleading stage) and *Donaleski* (an independent lawful source and an independent decisionmaker defeat causation) are built for this defense. A complaint filed now would most likely be dismissed and would teach the defendants the theory.

**There is no supported federal route today.** No federal claim is pleaded. Diversity cannot be alleged without citizenship facts, and the provable amount in controversy is far below $75,000 (§ 2). The federal draft is kept only as a conditional fallback.

**The packet contains a claim the draft missed, and it does not depend on proving registry provenance.** The "Universal Background Check Packet 2026.pdf" that HR sent on Sept. 22 (E-05; blank copy at V05 pp. 26–28) has three pages: a SHRM-template policy, an "Acknowledgment and Authorization," and a data-entry form. The authorization says Plaintiff received *"separate documents entitled DISCLOSURE REGARDING BACKGROUND INVESTIGATION, DISCLOSURE FOR INVESTIGATIVE CONSUMER REPORT…"* (V05 p.27). Those documents are not in the packet. Gmail has no message at all from Universal Background Screening, although other screening vendors' messages from the same period do appear (searched Nov. 12, 2026). If no standalone disclosure was ever provided before the report was obtained, that is a document-provable violation of ICRAA (Civ. Code § 1786.16) and FCRA (15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(2)(A)). ICRAA carries actual damages or $10,000, whichever is greater (Civ. Code § 1786.50(a)). *Parsonage* (2026) holds that a form violation alone gives standing in California state court. This is the **most valuable provable theory per unit of proof.**

## 2. Subject-matter jurisdiction (Rule 8(a)(1); Rules 12(b)(1), 12(b)(6))

**Conclusion: on this record no basis for federal jurisdiction is supported. This is flagged explicitly.**

### 2.1 § 1331 (federal question)
- Penal Code § 290.46 is state law. Citing *Staub* or Ninth Circuit cat's-paw cases does not create a federal claim (V07 p.2).
- **FCRA § 1681b(b)(2)(A)** is the only candidate. The elements are: (i) the employer procured or caused to be procured a consumer report for employment purposes; (ii) without first giving a clear and conspicuous disclosure in a document consisting solely of the disclosure, and/or without written authorization; (iii) willfully (§ 1681n, statutory damages of $100–$1,000) or negligently (§ 1681o, actual damages only).
  - Element (i) is **INFERENCE**. The offer was conditioned on a check, and the policy bars a first day before the check is complete (V05 pp.24, 26). Plaintiff then started work.
  - Element (ii) is **DOCUMENTED-in-part** (the packet as sent) and **UNKNOWN** (any portal).
  - **Standing risk:** *TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez*, 594 U.S. 413, 426–31 (2021), requires a concrete harm for damages. Bare disclosure-format violations are frequently dismissed for lack of standing after *TransUnion*. [I could not pull and verify specific N.D. Cal. post-*TransUnion* (b)(2) decisions in this session.] Ninth Circuit merits authority on the standalone requirement (*Syed v. M-I, LLC*, 853 F.3d 492 (9th Cir. 2017); *Gilberg v. California Check Cashing Stores, LLC*, 913 F.3d 1169 (9th Cir. 2019)) predates *TransUnion* on the standing question. [Pin cites unverified this session.]
  - **Rule 12(b)(1) outcome if standing fails:** dismissal without prejudice. The court would then likely decline supplemental jurisdiction over the novel § 290.46 question (28 U.S.C. § 1367(c)(1), (3)).
- **Title VII, ADA, ADEA, USERRA, § 1981:** no protected-basis facts appear anywhere in the record (V07 p.2). Nothing should be inferred from Plaintiff's name or anything else.

### 2.2 § 1332 (diversity / alienage)
The record contains **no** facts on any of these points, and none may be inferred from domicile or business registration (V01 p.1; V07 p.2):
- Plaintiff's citizenship. If he is a U.S. citizen, his state citizenship is his domicile. If he is not a citizen, what is his nationality, and is he a lawful permanent resident domiciled in California? (§ 1332(a)(2): no alienage jurisdiction between a state citizen and an LPR domiciled in the same state.)
- Each corporate defendant's state or country of incorporation **and** principal place of business (nerve center: *Hertz Corp. v. Friend*, 559 U.S. 77, 92–93 (2010)). California Secretary of State "C" numbers (C2059637, C1878751, C1523032 per V02 p.2) are issued to qualified foreign corporations as well as domestic ones, so they say nothing about where an entity is incorporated.
- Diversity must be pleaded affirmatively as citizenship, not residence: *Kanter v. Warner-Lambert Co.*, 265 F.3d 853, 857–58 (9th Cir. 2001).

**Amount in controversy fails independently.** Legal-certainty test: *St. Paul Mercury Indem. Co. v. Red Cab Co.*, 303 U.S. 283, 288–89 (1938). Punitive or exemplary damages count when state law allows them (*Gibson v. Chrysler Corp.*, 261 F.3d 927, 945 (9th Cir. 2001) [pin unverified]). Measured against the employer alone (claims against separate defendants cannot be aggregated to reach $75,000):

| Component | Supportable now |
|---|---|
| Lost wages to Nov. 14 | $510–$576 (§ 5) |
| § 290.46 additional amount | $250 to 3× actual, about $1,620 |
| § 290.46 exemplary damages **or** civil penalty | ≤ $25,000 (not automatic; see the ambiguity note in § 4.2) |
| ICRAA | $10,000 (if the violation is proven) |
| FCRA statutory | ≤ $1,000 (willful) |
| Attorney's fees | $0 while pro se (repo memo § 8) |
| Emotional distress | Undocumented. Nothing in the record beyond recalled humiliation during training (V02 p.1). |
| **Total** | **≈ $38,000**, which is less than $75,000 |

Reaching $75,000 would require roughly $37,000 or more in good-faith emotional-distress damages caused by the *registry use*, not by the training berating. The record does not support that. Brand size and foreign-incorporation assumptions are not used (V01 p.1).

### 2.3 § 1367 (supplemental jurisdiction)
- Available only if Count I (FCRA) survives on its own. Supplemental jurisdiction would cover the ICRAA and § 290.46 counts as part of the same case or controversy (§ 1367(a)).
- Discretionary dismissal is likely if FCRA falls away or the state issue is novel (§ 1367(c)). The § 290.46 "user" and provenance questions are novel (repo memo § 5).
- Article III also applies to state claims heard in federal court, so *Parsonage*'s state-standing rule does not carry ICRAA over the federal threshold.

### 2.4 Venue (if federal)
- 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(2): events in Santa Clara.
- N.D. Cal. Civil L.R. 3-2(c), (e): San Jose Division.
- Venue does not cure the jurisdiction problem.

### 2.5 Recommended forum
**Santa Clara County Superior Court, unlimited civil**, if the good-faith demand exceeds $35,000 (CCP § 85(a)). Limited civil allows one deposition per party and 35 combined written discovery requests (CCP §§ 94–95), which is too little for provenance discovery.

Advantages of state court:
- Doe practice with relation back (CCP § 474) for Howell, Kanwell, Marriott, and HHG.
- *Parsonage* standing for ICRAA.
- No removal hook, **provided FCRA is not pleaded.** An FCRA count makes the case removable under 28 U.S.C. §§ 1331, 1441(a).

Small claims (≤ $12,500, CCP § 116.221) is fast but has no discovery and caps recovery below the ICRAA-plus-penalty range. It is not recommended.

### 4.2 Analytical separations the handoff requires
1. **Merits vs. pleading.** *Staub*, *Poland*, *France*, *Reeves*, and *Donaleski* are merits or summary-judgment causation cases. *Iqbal*, *Soo Park*, and *Lerma* govern pleading. The complaint must plead facts that make provenance and use plausible. It does not have to prove the reporting chain.
2. **Direct vs. indirect registry provenance.** *Direct*: an agent viewed meganslaw.ca.gov. *Indirect*: a CRA report carried website-sourced data and the hotel acted on it (the Matter 26 UPS "SOURCE OF RECORD: CALIFORNIA SEX OFFENDER REGISTRY" pattern). Matter 44 has neither yet. The indirect route is the more provable one here, through the UBS file.
3. **Independent timecard evidence vs. tainted assessment.** Barros wrote that he "just checked your time card" (T-08). That is his own review of an objective record, the core of the *Donaleski* defense. The taint theory survives only if Howell's input set the *number* of shifts, separate from what the attendance record alone would justify.
4. **Source/use evidence vs. animus evidence.** The Oct. 29 remark is *animus or knowledge* evidence (*Reid* lets it count in context). It is not *source* evidence. A sex-offense remark alone does not identify a registry source (V07 p.1).
5. **Cat's paw under § 290.46's own standard.** Section 290.46 has no motive element and imposes no USERRA- or Title VII-style burden. Liability attaches to "any use … in violation of paragraph (2)" by "the user." That means:
   - If Howell used website information for an employment purpose (assessing a trainee), **he is arguably the user**. The employer is liable vicariously (*Lisa M.* scope-of-employment test), not through cat's paw.
   - Cat's-paw-style proximate cause matters **only for actual damages** (lost shifts). Civ. Code § 3333 is the tort measure.
   - The $250 floor and the penalty arguably attach to the use itself even if causation of the lost shifts fails. [Interpretation; no controlling authority located.]
6. **Remedy text ambiguity.** "exemplary damages, or a civil penalty not exceeding twenty-five thousand dollars" can be read with the $25,000 cap modifying only the civil penalty.
