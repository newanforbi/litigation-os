FIRST CAUSE OF ACTION

Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(2)(A) — Against Employer

27. Plaintiff incorporates paragraphs 1 through 24.

28. Employer procured, or caused to be procured, a consumer report about Plaintiff for employment purposes.

29. Before doing so, Employer did not provide Plaintiff a clear and conspicuous written disclosure, in a document consisting solely of the disclosure, that a consumer report may be obtained for employment purposes, as 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(2)(A)(i) requires.

30. Employer acted willfully, or at minimum in reckless disregard of its statutory duty. Safeco Ins. Co. of Am. v. Burr, 551 U.S. 47, 57, 69 (2007). The statute "unambiguously requires" a standalone disclosure. Syed v. M-I, LLC, 853 F.3d 492, 500 (9th Cir. 2017). An employer that provides no disclosure at all disregards the statute's plain text, and "[a]n interpretation of the FCRA that contradicts plain statutory text is objectively unreasonable." Grijalva v. ADP Screening & Selection Servs., 151 F.4th 1055, 1060–61 (9th Cir. 2025). Employer's own Acknowledgment recites the existence of separate FCRA disclosure documents, which shows awareness of the requirement, yet the packet it transmitted did not contain them. In the alternative, Employer acted negligently. 15 U.S.C. § 1681o.

31. Plaintiff suffered the concrete injuries alleged in paragraph 13. He is entitled to statutory damages of not less than $100 and not more than $1,000, punitive damages, and costs under 15 U.S.C. § 1681n(a), or, in the alternative, actual damages and costs under 15 U.S.C. § 1681o.

SECOND CAUSE OF ACTION [Conditional: requires ¶ 25]

Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1681b(b)(3)(A) — Against Employer

32. Plaintiff incorporates paragraphs 1 through 26.

33. Reducing Plaintiff's scheduled paid shifts was a "decision for employment purposes that adversely affects" a current employee and therefore an "adverse action." 15 U.S.C. § 1681a(k)(1)(B)(ii).

34. On information and belief, Employer took that adverse action based in whole or in part on the consumer report, including its sex-offender registry entry (¶ 25).

35. Before taking that action, Employer did not provide Plaintiff a copy of the report or the summary of rights prescribed under 15 U.S.C. § 1681g(c)(3), as § 1681b(b)(3)(A) requires. Plaintiff therefore had no opportunity to review the report, or to dispute or explain its use, before his shifts were reduced.

36. Plaintiff's injury is concrete. He lost paid shifts and wages (¶¶ 15, 24, 44), with the consequences alleged in paragraph 24, and he was denied the opportunity the statute guarantees to respond before that loss. Employer's violation was willful (¶ 30) or, in the alternative, negligent. Plaintiff is entitled to actual damages, or statutory damages of $100 to $1,000, together with punitive damages and costs. 15 U.S.C. §§ 1681n(a), 1681o.

THIRD CAUSE OF ACTION

Investigative Consumer Reporting Agencies Act, Cal. Civ. Code § 1786.16 — Against Employer

37. Plaintiff incorporates paragraphs 1 through 13.

38. Employer caused an investigative consumer report about Plaintiff to be prepared or procured for employment purposes without first providing the clear and conspicuous standalone written disclosure required by California Civil Code § 1786.16(a)(2)(B) [verify subdivision]. That disclosure must identify the investigative consumer reporting agency, the nature and scope of the investigation, and the permissible purpose of the report. Employer also failed to provide a box to check to receive a copy of the report, as § 1786.16(b)(1) [verify] requires.

39. Plaintiff suffered the concrete injuries alleged in paragraph 13. He is entitled to his actual damages or $10,000, whichever is greater, and costs under California Civil Code § 1786.50(a). Because the violation was grossly negligent or willful, he is also entitled to punitive damages under § 1786.50(b).

FOURTH CAUSE OF ACTION [Conditional: requires ¶¶ 25–26]

Cal. Penal Code § 290.46(j)(2)(E), (j)(4)(A) — Against Employer and Does 1–10

40. Plaintiff incorporates paragraphs 1 through 26.

41. Section 290.46(j)(2)(E) prohibits the use of information disclosed under § 290.46 for purposes relating to employment, except as authorized by § 290.46(j)(1) or another provision of law. Section 290.46(j)(4)(A) makes "the user" liable for prohibited use.

42. On information and belief, information disclosed under § 290.46 was used for purposes relating to Plaintiff's employment. It was used in assessing Plaintiff's suitability for continued Night Audit work, and in deciding the number of shifts assigned to him (¶¶ 25–26). Howell, acting within the scope of his employment as Plaintiff's assigned trainer, conveyed an assessment influenced by that information to Barros, and Barros relied on it. Employer is liable for that use.

43. No exception applies. The use was not made to protect a person at risk: Employer continued to assign Plaintiff to overnight work with guests and staff. No other provision of law authorizes a hotel's use of Megan's Law website information in scheduling a front-desk employee.

44. As a result of the use and the scheduling decisions it influenced, Plaintiff lost paid shifts. Measured against his actual pre-reduction weeks (¶ 15), those losses are estimated through November 14, 2026 at about $533 to $655 in gross wages, and they continue to accrue according to proof. Plaintiff has also suffered emotional distress from the resulting threat to his housing (¶ 24). He will also incur any consequential losses actually and proximately caused by the lost wages, according to proof.
