#### II. LEGAL STANDARD

Under Civil L.R. 7-3(d), once a reply is filed, no additional memoranda may be filed without prior Court approval. The Ninth Circuit recognizes Civil L.R. 7-3(d) as the proper mechanism for a non-moving party to seek leave to file a sur-reply when a moving party injects new matter in a reply brief. *Dutta v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co.*, 895 F.3d 1166, 1171–72 (9th Cir. 2018); *see also Zamani v. Carnes*, 491 F.3d 990, 997 (9th Cir. 2007) ("The district court need not consider arguments raised for the first time in a reply brief."). Courts in this District routinely find good cause for a brief sur-reply "where defendants have raised a new argument or evidence in a reply brief" to which the opposing party has had no opportunity to respond. *In re Cloudera, Inc. Sec. Litig.*, No. 19-CV-03221-LHK, 2021 WL 2115303, at *8 n.2 (N.D. Cal. May 25, 2021).

#### III. GOOD CAUSE EXISTS FOR A NARROW FOUR-PAGE SUR-REPLY

Plaintiff does not seek to re-argue his Opposition. Rather, Defendants' Reply introduces four specific new arguments, procedural requests, and record mischaracterizations that were absent from Defendants' opening brief (Dkt. 22) and could not have been addressed in Plaintiff's Opposition (Dkt. 27):

1. **New Bar-to-Amendment Argument Under *Airs Aromatics* (Dkt. 33 at 5, 9).** For the first time on reply, Defendants invoke *Airs Aromatics, LLC v. Victoria's Secret Stores Brand Management, Inc.*, 744 F.3d 595, 600 (9th Cir. 2014), to argue that Plaintiff is legally barred from amending FAC ¶ 16 to conform to the CHP dashcam sequence (showing that the officers' knock woke Plaintiff and Officer Melano opened the passenger door three seconds after saying "open the window"). Plaintiff has had no opportunity to address *Airs Aromatics* or cite controlling Ninth Circuit authority—*PAE Government Services, Inc. v. MPRI, Inc.*, 514 F.3d 856, 860 (9th Cir. 2007)—establishing that the Federal Rules do not prohibit correcting an earlier allegation in good faith to align with an objective recording.
2. **New Assertion That Rule 12(b)(6) Permits Drawing Inferences Against Plaintiff Whenever an Innocent Explanation Exists (Dkt. 33 at 2–3, 7).** Defendants' Reply newly asserts—citing *Bell Atlantic Corp. v. Twombly*, 550 U.S. 544, 555 (2007), and *Ashcroft v. Iqbal*, 556 U.S. 662, 667 (2009)—that on a Rule 12(b)(6) motion "the Court is free to draw all reasonable inferences from the facts alleged, whether such inferences are 'for' or 'against' Plaintiff's theory of liability," and that a complaint must be dismissed whenever its allegations are "also consistent with an innocent explanation." That newly asserted standard directly conflicts with binding Ninth Circuit precedent in *Usher v. City of Los Angeles*, 828 F.2d 556, 561 (9th Cir. 1987), and *Starr v. Baca*, 652 F.3d 1202, 1216 (9th Cir. 2011).
3. **New Mischaracterization of Plaintiff's Form-Label Clarification as a Factual Concession of Alcohol Odor (Dkt. 33 at 6, 8).** In his Opposition, Plaintiff candidly noted that a CHP form field reads "odor of an alcoholic beverage" rather than referring to a container. Defendants' Reply converts that textual clarification into an affirmative factual concession that Officer Meza-Gonzalez actually detected an odor of alcohol, deploying it as an undisputed pillar of probable cause and qualified immunity despite FAC ¶ 26's explicit factual denial.
4. **New Request to Convert Defendants' Rule 12(f) Motion to Strike Punitive Damages into a Rule 12(b)(6) Motion to Dismiss (Dkt. 33 at 10).** Defendants' opening brief sought to strike punitive damages exclusively under Rule 12(f). After Plaintiff pointed out that *Whittlestone, Inc. v. Handi-Craft Co.*, 618 F.3d 970, 974–75 (9th Cir. 2010), forecloses Rule 12(f) strikes of damages prayers, Defendants asked for the first time on reply that the Court recharacterize their strike motion as a Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss punitive damages as a "separate claim." Plaintiff is entitled to respond briefly to that newly requested procedural conversion and to clarify the governing *Smith v. Wade*, 461 U.S. 30, 56 (1983), recklessness standard.

Granting leave causes no prejudice and no delay. The Motion to Dismiss hearing is nearly four weeks away (December 18, 2026), Plaintiff's proposed Sur-Reply is attached now and limited to four pages, and Defendants themselves filed their Reply four days past the Court's deadline without leave.
