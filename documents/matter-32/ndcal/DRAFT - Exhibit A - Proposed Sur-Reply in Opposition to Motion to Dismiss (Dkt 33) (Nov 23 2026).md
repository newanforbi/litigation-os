Plaintiff Brendan Ngwa Nforbi submits this Sur-Reply, limited strictly to five matters raised for the first time in Defendants' Reply (Dkt. 33).

#### I. THE NEW DOOR-ENTRY IMMUNITY THEORY ASSUMES UNPLEADED FACTS.

The reply's immunity question assumes an "unconscious" driver. (Dkt. 33 at 4:28–5:3, 8:5–8.) The FAC alleges a fatigued driver resting asleep in a normal posture in an undamaged car off the travel lanes. (FAC ¶¶ 11, 14, 16, 56.) The Court accepts those facts and draws inferences in Plaintiff's favor; where both sides offer plausible explanations, the claim survives. *Starr v. Baca*, 652 F.3d 1202, 1216 (9th Cir. 2011).

On those facts, the law was clearly established in November 2024. Opening a car door and entering the interior is a search requiring probable cause or another particularized justification. *United States v. Ngumezi*, 980 F.3d 1285, 1288–89 (9th Cir. 2020). The emergency-aid exception requires "an objectively reasonable basis for believing" someone inside needs immediate aid. *Brigham City v. Stuart*, 547 U.S. 398, 406 (2006); *Hopkins v. Bonvicino*, 573 F.3d 752, 763–64 (9th Cir. 2009). The reply distinguishes *Hopkins* by asserting the officers had "no information," yet the FAC alleges what they saw through the windows with flashlights: an intact car and a person in a resting posture. (FAC ¶¶ 11, 14, 16.) Sleep in a parked car is not an emergency.

The reply also relies on half of Plaintiff's proposed amendment: that he did not respond to "open the window" before the door opened. (Dkt. 33 at 4:14–16.) The same proposed allegation states that the officers' knock woke Plaintiff, Officer Melano said "open the window," and Melano opened the door three seconds later, "before Plaintiff could comply," asking "Are you okay?" only after the door was open. (Dkt. 27 at 19:26–20:3 (recording at 00:56:40–00:56:43).) An officer speaking to an awake driver just roused by a knock who shows no sign of distress has no objective basis for emergency aid. On either version of the facts, the new immunity argument fails.

#### II. AIRS AROMATICS DOES NOT BAR CONFORMING PARAGRAPH 16.

"[T]here is nothing in the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure to prevent a party from filing successive pleadings that make inconsistent or even contradictory allegations." *PAE Gov't Servs., Inc. v. MPRI, Inc.*, 514 F.3d 856, 860 (9th Cir. 2007). Only a sham pleading is barred, and inconsistency alone does not show one. *Id.* at 858–60. The proposed correction conforms paragraph 16 to timestamps on the recording both sides possess—reflecting that the knock woke Plaintiff before Melano pulled the door open. It is not a change of theory to escape dismissal and does not change the result: on either version, the officers lacked an objective basis for emergency aid. Defendants cannot rely on the knock-and-command sequence to defend the entry (Dkt. 33 at 4:14–16) while arguing Plaintiff may not plead it accurately.

#### III. LEAVE TO AMEND COUNT II IS WARRANTED ON SPECIFIC FACTS.

The reply contends Plaintiff identified no specific fabricated statement. (Dkt. 33 at 2:9–13.) Facts raised in opposition papers are considered when deciding leave to amend. *Broam v. Bogan*, 320 F.3d 1023, 1026 n.2 (9th Cir. 2003). Deliberate fabrication requires deliberate fabrication of evidence causing a deprivation of liberty. *Spencer v. Peters*, 857 F.3d 789, 798 (9th Cir. 2017). The FAC already pleads the facts an amended Count II would set out element by element:

- **(a) Statements.** Meza-Gonzalez's DS-367 and arrest report state that Plaintiff's speech was "slurred," that he "seemed very confused and disoriented," and that there was a "strong distinct odor of an alcoholic beverage." (FAC ¶ 26(a)–(c).)
- **(b) Contrary record.** The recording captures extended coherent conversation—Plaintiff spelling his surname, discussing the car's mechanics, and correctly identifying his location—and over two hours of audio with no officer remarking on any odor. (*Id.* ¶¶ 20, 26(a)–(c).)
- **(c) Knowledge.** Meza-Gonzalez was present for everything he described. (*Id.* ¶¶ 40–41, 46.) *See Chism v. Washington State*, 661 F.3d 380, 388 (9th Cir. 2011).
- **(d) Deprivation.** The documents were used to obtain the blood-draw warrant (FAC ¶¶ 29, 36(b)), supported the parole hold extending Plaintiff's confinement (*id.* ¶ 36(a)), and formed the statutory basis for a one-year administrative suspension of his Class A commercial license (*id.* ¶¶ 36(d), 37). No criminal charge is required where fabrication causes the loss of a protected interest. *Costanich v. Dep't of Soc. & Health Servs.*, 627 F.3d 1101, 1108 (9th Cir. 2010).

It is not "absolutely clear" amendment cannot cure any defect. *Akhtar v. Mesa*, 698 F.3d 1202, 1212 (9th Cir. 2012). As to Count VI, Plaintiff does not seek leave to amend at this time and asks only that any dismissal be without prejudice; the refusal of the voluntary preliminary alcohol screening test remains part of Count III.

#### IV. CORRECTING A FORM LABEL IS NOT A CONCESSION OF ODOR.

The reply treats Plaintiff's statement that a CHP form field refers to "an odor of an alcoholic beverage, not to a container" as support for probable cause and immunity. (Dkt. 33 at 6:7–9, 8:9–13.) Plaintiff corrected what the form says, not whether the entry was true; FAC ¶ 26(a) alleges it is false. "[I]t is improper to assume the truth of an incorporated document" to dispute well-pleaded facts. *Khoja v. Orexigen Therapeutics, Inc.*, 899 F.3d 988, 1003 (9th Cir. 2018).

#### V. THE PUNITIVE-DAMAGES PRAYER IS NOT A CLAIM.

Recasting the Rule 12(f) strike as a Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal is new and may be disregarded. *Zamani v. Carnes*, 491 F.3d 990, 997 (9th Cir. 2007). *Whittlestone, Inc. v. Handi-Craft Co.*, 618 F.3d 970, 974–75 (9th Cir. 2010), bars the same result under another label. A prayer is a "demand for the relief sought," Fed. R. Civ. P. 8(a)(3), not a claim; its availability follows the surviving claims. Under § 1983, punitive damages require "reckless or callous indifference to the federally protected rights of others." *Smith v. Wade*, 461 U.S. 30, 56 (1983). The reply's "Both cannot be true" point fails: speculating aloud that a cup holds alcohol does not justify entry, *Ngumezi*, 980 F.3d at 1288–89, and asking "Are you okay?" afterward cannot validate it.

#### VI. CONCLUSION

The Court should disregard or reject the reply's new arguments, deny dismissal of Counts I, III, IV, and V and the motion to strike, dismiss Count II with leave to amend and Count VI without prejudice, and grant leave to amend as to any other defect.
